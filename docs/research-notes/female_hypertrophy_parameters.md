# Hypertrophy Programming Parameters for a 22-Year-Old Woman (2–5 gym sessions/week, variable length)

Evidence grades used throughout: **[A]** strong (several meta-analyses or a position stand agree); **[B]** moderate (one meta-analysis or meta-regression with wide uncertainty, or several consistent RCTs); **[C]** weak (one small RCT, acute or mechanistic data); **[O]** expert opinion, coach surveys or practitioner convention. Method note: WebFetch could not reach sportrxiv.org, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov or peerj.com in this session (DNS blocked), so most numbers below come from abstracts and search-indexed summaries rather than full texts. Where only a secondary source reported a number, it is flagged. Research current to Nov 2026.

## 1. Sex differences: relative hypertrophy, strength, fatigability and recovery

### Takeaway
Women gain about the same *relative* muscle size as men from the same program. Men gain slightly more *absolute* size, and only in the upper body. Women gain relatively more upper-body strength (mostly in untrained samples). Women are less fatigable in isometric and slow contractions, but the advantage shrinks or disappears in fast dynamic work. Reps-to-failure at a given %1RM do not differ by sex. The same hypertrophy parameters as for men apply. At most, rest on isolation work can be slightly shorter, and the menstrual cycle should not drive programming.

### Cited Findings
- **Roberts, Nuckols & Krieger 2020** (JSCR 34(5):1448–1460) meta-analysis. Hypertrophy showed no meaningful sex difference (ES 0.07, P = 0.31; 12 outcomes, 10 studies). Upper-body strength gains favored females (ES −0.60, P = 0.002; 19 outcomes). Lower-body strength showed no difference (23 outcomes). Most included participants were untrained. — [PubMed 32218059](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32218059/)
- **Refalo et al. 2025** (PeerJ 13:e19042, Bayesian meta-analysis). It included 29 studies of adults aged 18–45 on identical programs. Absolute hypertrophy slightly favored males (SMD 0.19, 95% HDI 0.11–0.28). Relative gains were similar between sexes. — [DOAJ abstract](https://doaj.org/article/dbf856c9d64840b59318c009c4abd626)
- In Refalo 2025, the absolute sex difference appeared only for upper-body regions. Type I fibre growth slightly favored males and Type II fibre growth was similar. Training experience did not moderate outcomes. The authors conclude that "females appear to have a similar capacity for muscle hypertrophy to males, particularly when relative changes from baseline are considered." — [Edinburgh Napier record](https://www.napier.ac.uk/research-and-innovation/research-search/outputs/sex-differences-in-absolute-and-relative-changes-in-muscle-size-following-resistance); [PeerJ](https://peerj.com/articles/19042)
- **Nuzzo, Pinto, Nosaka & Steele 2024** (Sports Med 54(2):303–321). The analysis pooled 952 reps-to-failure tests from 7,289 people in 269 studies. Sex, age and training status "did not clearly moderate" the reps–%1RM relationship. Exercise did: the leg press allows more reps than the bench press at the same %1RM. Between-person variation is larger at lower %1RM. — [Solent repository](https://pure.solent.ac.uk/en/publications/maximal-number-of-repetitions-at-percentages-of-the-one-repetitio/); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10933212)
- **Hunter 2014** (Acta Physiologica) review. The sex difference in fatigability is *task-specific*: it depends on contraction type, intensity, speed, muscle group and environment. Women are more fatigue-resistant than men for most muscle groups, at least in isometric fatiguing contractions. — [PubMed 24433272](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24433272/)
- **Hunter 2016** (Exp Physiol 101(2):250–255). The sex difference in fatigue during repeated *dynamic* contractions is diminished at relatively high velocity, and power loss in fast low-load (20% MVIC) contractions was similar between sexes. A sex difference persists for repeated *slow*-velocity contractions. — [PMC5777316](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5777316)
- **2024 study in resistance-trained men and women** (search summary). Within-set fatigue was similar in the squat, but women recovered better between sets in both the squat and isokinetic knee extension. Women showed *more* within-set fatigue on the single-joint knee extension, and aerobic capacity was only weakly associated with recovery. — [PubMed 38347629 (record surfaced by search; full text not opened)](https://www.pubmed.gov/38347629/)
- **Counter-evidence (Millender study, reported secondarily)**: rest length mattered as much for women as for men, and short rests reduced total volume lifted. — [Triathlete](https://www.triathlete.com/training/should-women-use-different-weight-training-rest-intervals-than-men/)
- **Colenso-Semple et al. 2023** umbrella review. It is "premature to conclude that short-term fluctuations in reproductive hormones appreciably influence acute exercise performance or longer-term strength or hypertrophic adaptations". The review notes poor methods in the literature and says the case for follicular-phase-based training rests on "few papers". — [Frontiers Sports Act Living (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/6b0c0ed2b14045e29d01c055be956349)

### Inferences
- Use the same volume, load and RIR framework as for men **[A]**. Do not cut volume or push women to "high reps, light weights" for toning. The reps–%1RM relationship is the same, so standard load–rep tables apply **[B]**.
- Do not neglect the upper body. Absolute upper-body gains are the only place a sex gap shows up, and relative upper-body strength gains can be large early on **[B]**. This argues for keeping back and shoulder volume at full building levels rather than letting glute and leg work crowd it out (inference).
- Women's fatigue resistance is probably most relevant to isolation and slower-tempo work. It could justify slightly shorter rests (about 60–90 s) on isolation exercises and tolerance of slightly higher set counts. No RCT has tested sex-specific volume or rest prescriptions, so treat this as [C]/[O].
- Do not periodize by menstrual phase **[B]**. Allow a symptom-based "readiness" adjustment, such as reducing sets or adding RIR on bad days, as an individual feature **[O]**.

### Gaps
- I found no RCT that assigns women versus men to different volumes, RIR or rest periods. Any sex-specific programming beyond "same rules" is extrapolated from acute fatigue data.
- Whether women recover faster *between sessions* (e.g., less muscle damage, faster strength recovery at 24–72 h) was not resolved by the sources retrieved. A 2014 review (Vieira & Gadelha) reportedly found faster recovery but noted confounding by absolute strength differences. It was not opened.
- The user's "Refalo et al. 2023" sex-difference reference appears to correspond to the Refalo et al. PeerJ 2025 paper, likely an earlier preprint. The preprint date was not verified.
- Very few studies include highly trained women. Refalo 2025 calls for research in highly trained participants.

## 2. Weekly volume per muscle group

### Takeaway
More weekly sets produce more growth, with diminishing returns and no hard ceiling detected. ≥10 sets/muscle/week is the evidence-based floor for maximizing growth (ACSM 2026). 12–20 sets is a good working range for trained lifters, and gains per extra set shrink beyond about 20. Count indirect (synergist) sets as 0.5. Cap any one muscle at about 10–11 fractional sets per session and spread the rest across days.

### Cited Findings
- **ACSM 2026 Position Stand** (MSSE; overview of 137 systematic reviews, >30,000 participants). "Muscle hypertrophy was enhanced by higher volumes (≥10 sets/wk) and eccentric overload." Training to momentary failure, equipment type, set structure, time under tension and periodization "did not consistently impact training outcomes." — [Newswise/ACSM release](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years); [ACSM infographic](https://acsm.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Position-Stand-infographic.pdf)
- **Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017** meta-analysis (15 studies, 34 treatment groups). It found a graded dose-response between weekly sets and hypertrophy but could not inform volumes above about 10 sets/week. One secondary blog puts the gain at about 0.37% muscle per extra weekly set (treat cautiously). — [Baz-Valle 2022 (discusses Schoenfeld 2017), PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8884877/); [getfitcraft (secondary)](https://getfitcraft.com/science/how-many-sets-per-muscle-group-per-week-research)
- **Baz-Valle et al. 2022** (J Hum Kinet) systematic review. It compared moderate (12–20) with high (>20) weekly sets and concluded that 12–20 weekly sets per muscle "may be an optimum standard recommendation" for hypertrophy *in young trained men*. Secondary summaries say >20 sets beat 12–20 only for the triceps. — [PMC8884877](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8884877/)
- **Pelland et al. 2025** (Sports Med, online Dec 2025, doi 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w; Bayesian meta-regressions of 67 studies). "Fractional" set counting, where indirect sets count 0.5, best predicted hypertrophy and strength. The probability of a positive volume effect was 100% for both outcomes. Strength shows more pronounced diminishing returns than hypertrophy. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398333408_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_Response_Meta-Regressions_Exploring_the_Effects_of_Weekly_Volume_and_Frequency_on_Muscle_Hypertrophy_and_Strength_Gains_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_ResponseJ_C_Pelland_et_al)
- Pelland 2025 (search summary of full text): there is "no plateau albeit with diminishing returns". It estimates about +0.24% hypertrophy per additional weekly set at the average volume (95% CrI 0.15–0.33). One summary describes a "practical plateau" at about 31 fractional sets/week, which conflicts with the paper's own no-plateau framing. — [ResearchGate (as above)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398333408_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_Response_Meta-Regressions_Exploring_the_Effects_of_Weekly_Volume_and_Frequency_on_Muscle_Hypertrophy_and_Strength_Gains_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_ResponseJ_C_Pelland_et_al)
- **Remmert, Pelland et al. (per-session volume preprint, 2025)**. Superiority becomes undetectable at about 2 *direct* sets per session for strength and about 11 *fractional* sets per session for hypertrophy. Above 11 sets, hypertrophy still increases "in a decreasing manner". Data at very high per-session volumes are insufficient. — [SportRxiv 537](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537)

### Inferences
- Use **fractional sets** for the weekly ledger: a direct set counts 1.0 for the prime mover and 0.5 for each meaningful synergist (Pelland method) **[B]**.
- **Floor**: ≥10 fractional sets/muscle/week for muscles she wants to grow **[A]**.
- **Working range**: 10–20 fractional sets/week **[A/B]**. Diminishing returns mean the 15th–20th set adds less than the 5th–10th, so add volume only when recovery and adherence allow. Default ceiling ≈20–24 direct sets for priority muscles; >25 is unsupported for a non-competitor **[B/O]**.
- **Per-session cap**: ≈10–11 fractional sets per muscle **[B]**. When the weekly target needs more, add a session that trains that muscle rather than stacking sets.
- **Starting point (inference)**: beginners 8–12 fractional sets/muscle/week; intermediates 12–16; progress by +1–2 sets/muscle/week per 1–2 weeks within a mesocycle, then reset at deload (RP/Israetel-style volume ramp **[O]**).

### Gaps
- No volume dose-response meta-analysis is specific to women. Baz-Valle's 12–20 range is from young trained men. Pelland's training-status and sex moderator results were not retrievable here.
- The exact Pelland curve values (predicted % growth at 5/10/20/30 sets) could not be extracted because the full text was blocked.

## 3. Frequency and split structure for 2–5 variable sessions/week

### Takeaway
When weekly volume is equal, frequency barely affects hypertrophy. Frequency still matters in practice, because the per-session cap (~11 fractional sets/muscle) means higher weekly volumes need ≥2 exposures, and strength improves with frequency. Train every muscle ≥2×/week. For a trainee whose session count and length vary, a rolling full-body or upper/lower system driven by a weekly per-muscle set ledger is the most robust structure.

### Cited Findings
- **Schoenfeld et al. 2019** (J Sports Sci; 25 studies, 836 participants, 6–30 weeks). There was no significant difference between higher and lower frequency when volume was equated. Non-volume-equated studies modestly favored higher frequency (1 vs 3+ days/week). — [PubMed 30558493](https://www.pubmed.gov/30558493/)
- **Schoenfeld et al. 2016** (Sports Med; 10 studies). Twice-weekly training beat once-weekly when volume was equated. The 2019 analysis with more studies supersedes this. — [PubMed 27102172](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/)
- **Pelland 2025**: evidence for a frequency effect on hypertrophy was weak and compatible with negligible effects. For strength, the posterior probability was 100% that gains increase with frequency, with diminishing returns. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398333408_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_Response_Meta-Regressions_Exploring_the_Effects_of_Weekly_Volume_and_Frequency_on_Muscle_Hypertrophy_and_Strength_Gains_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_ResponseJ_C_Pelland_et_al)
- **ACSM 2026**: train all major muscle groups at least 2 days/week and increase gradually. — [Newswise/ACSM](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years)

### Inferences
- **Frequency per muscle**: 2×/week minimum **[A]**; 3×/week when weekly target >~16–20 fractional sets or sessions are short **[B]** (per-session cap).
- **Split by sessions available (inference/[O])**:
  - 2 days: Full-body A / Full-body B.
  - 3 days: Full-body A/B/C. Alternatively, glute- or lower-emphasis twice plus upper once, provided every muscle still gets ≥2 exposures.
  - 4 days: Upper/Lower/Upper/Lower or 4× full-body.
  - 5 days: Upper/Lower/Upper/Lower/Full, or Lower(glute)/Upper/Lower(quad-ham)/Upper/Full-body priority session.
- **Variable schedule**: don't fix a split. Keep a rolling 7-day ledger of fractional sets per muscle. Each booked session picks the muscles with the largest remaining deficit, excluding any muscle trained to ≤2 RIR in the last ~48 h. The 48 h buffer is [O]; there is no direct evidence for an exact recovery window.

### Gaps
- No study directly tests "unpredictable" scheduling. The ledger approach is an engineering inference from the volume and frequency literature.
- No sex-specific frequency trials were found.

## 4. Load and rep ranges

### Takeaway
Hypertrophy is similar across a wide loading spectrum (roughly 30–85% 1RM, i.e., about 6–30 reps) *if sets are taken close to failure*. Heavier loads build more maximal strength. Default to 6–12 reps on compounds and 10–20 on isolation/machine work, with occasional 15–30 on small muscles. Lighter loads must be taken closer to failure, and RIR guesses are least accurate on high-rep sets.

### Cited Findings
- **Schoenfeld, Grgic, Ogborn & Krieger 2017** (21 studies; ≤60% vs >60% 1RM; all sets to failure; ≥6 weeks). "Maximal strength benefits are obtained from the use of heavy loads while muscle hypertrophy can be equally achieved across a spectrum of loading ranges." — [PubMed 28834797](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/)
- **Grgic & Schoenfeld 2020** fiber-level meta-analysis: no significant low- vs high-load difference in type I or type II fiber hypertrophy, with very wide intervals. — [PubMed 33312275](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33312275/)
- **Nuzzo et al. 2024**: the classic table (100% = 1, 90% = 4, 85% = 6, 80% = 8 reps) has been updated with exercise-specific tables. The leg press yields more reps than the bench press at the same %1RM, sex does not moderate the relationship, and variability is larger at low %1RM. — [PMC10933212](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10933212); [Solent](https://pure.solent.ac.uk/en/publications/maximal-number-of-repetitions-at-percentages-of-the-one-repetitio/)
- Robinson 2024 commentary: "the heavier the load, the less of an impact going to failure had on the hypertrophy stimulus." — [News-Medical summary](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20240801/New-study-reveals-sweet-spot-for-muscle-growth.aspx)
- **Halperin et al. 2022** (Sports Med): "more repetitions to failure was associated with lower [RIR] accuracy." — [BVS/MEDLINE 34542869](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/ses/resource/pt/mdl-34542869)

### Inferences
- **Allowed hypertrophy rep range**: 5–30 reps per set **[A]**.
- **Defaults by exercise class [B/O]**:
  - Heavy compounds (squat, deadlift variants, presses, rows): 6–10 (allow 5–8 blocks for strength emphasis).
  - Machine and cable compounds: 8–15.
  - Isolation (curls, extensions, raises, leg curl/extension, abduction): 10–20.
  - Small or high-fatigue-resistant muscles (lateral delts, calves, rear delts, abs): 12–30.
- Because RIR judgement is worse at high reps, prefer machines or cables for sets above 15 reps and cap them at ~25–30 reps (inference from Halperin and Robinson).
- Because women have small absolute loads, the smallest available jump (e.g., 2 kg dumbbell steps on a 10 kg dumbbell = +20%) is often too large. The generator should use rep progression (section 8) and rep ranges wide enough (e.g., 8–15) to absorb load jumps (inference).

### Gaps
- The Lopez et al. 2021 loading meta-analysis and Schoenfeld's 2021 loading recommendations were not retrieved. The "6–30 rep" equivalence statement rests on Schoenfeld 2017 plus the mapping of ≤60%/>60% 1RM to reps via Nuzzo 2024.
- The IUSCA 2021 hypertrophy position stand (Schoenfeld, Fisher et al.) was located but its specific rep and rest numbers could not be extracted. — [IUSCA](https://journal.iusca.org/index.php/Journal/article/view/81)

## 5. Proximity to failure (RIR)

### Takeaway
Hypertrophy improves as sets end closer to failure, non-linearly. Strength is largely insensitive to RIR. Going all the way to momentary failure adds little or nothing over 1–2 RIR in trained lifters, but it costs more fatigue. Practical targets are 1–3 RIR on compounds and 0–2 RIR on isolation and machines. Self-reported RIR runs about 1 rep conservative (people underpredict how many reps they have left).

### Cited Findings
- **Robinson et al. 2024** (Sports Med 54(9):2209–2231; meta-regressions). Hypertrophy improves as sets end closer to failure, with slopes whose confidence intervals exclude zero, while strength gains are similar across a wide RIR range. The authors called the analysis exploratory, model fit was modest, and the exact RIR–hypertrophy relationship "remains unclear". — [Abertay record](https://rke.abertay.ac.uk/en/publications/exploring-the-dose-response-relationship-between-estimated-resist/); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/07/240731140409.htm)
- Robinson 2024 practical ranges (secondary summary): about 0–5 RIR for hypertrophy and about 3–5 RIR adequate for strength. Some reviewers emphasize 0–2 RIR. — [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20240801/New-study-reveals-sweet-spot-for-muscle-growth.aspx)
- **Refalo et al. 2023** (Sports Med) meta-analysis of 15 studies. The overall set-failure effect was trivial (ES 0.19). With momentary failure strictly defined, there was no advantage (ES 0.12, CI crossing 0). — [AUT repository](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/bitstreams/9e3df52b-a8b3-4139-b172-feefd51e5334/download); [Duke/Trexler record](https://fds.duke.edu/db/aas/faculty/eric.trexler/publications/384901)
- **Refalo et al. 2024** (J Sports Sci 42(1):85–101) within-subject RCT in 12 trained men and 6 trained women, 8 weeks, 2 sessions/week of leg press plus leg extension. FAIL and 1–2 RIR produced equal quadriceps thickness gains: +0.181 cm (HDI 0.119–0.243) vs +0.182 cm (HDI 0.115–0.247). FAIL produced consistently more velocity and rep loss (fatigue). — [PubMed 38393985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38393985/)
- **Halperin et al. 2022** (Sports Med; 262 effect sizes). Participants underpredicted reps to failure by about 0.95 reps (I² = 97.9%). Accuracy slightly improved closer to failure, training status did not moderate accuracy, and between-person SD was 1.45 reps. — [BVS/MEDLINE](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/ses/resource/pt/mdl-34542869); [IUCC record](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/accuracy-in-predicting-repetitions-to-task-failure-in-resistance-/)
- **ACSM 2026**: training to momentary failure did not consistently affect outcomes. — [Newswise/ACSM](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years)

### Inferences
- **RIR targets [B]**:
  - Free-weight compounds with technical or injury risk (barbell squat, deadlift/RDL, barbell bench, overhead press): 2–3 RIR on early sets, 1–2 on the last set; never 0.
  - Machine/Smith/cable compounds (leg press, hack squat, chest press, pulldown, rows): 1–2 RIR; 0–1 allowed on the last set.
  - Isolation: 0–2 RIR; last set 0 (technical failure) allowed.
  - Light loads (>15 reps): 0–1 RIR. Low-load sets lose more stimulus when far from failure.
- **Mesocycle RIR ramp [O]** (RP-style): week 1 ≈3 RIR → final week ≈0–1 RIR → deload at ≥4 RIR.
- **Calibration**: logged RIR is probably about 1 rep conservative, so a logged "2 RIR" may really be ~3. Every 3–6 weeks, schedule an AMRAP on the last set of one safe machine or isolation exercise per muscle. Use the error (actual reps − predicted reps) to correct her future RIR logs. This is an inference from Halperin.
- Beginners: start at 2–3 RIR on compounds for the first ~4–8 weeks while technique stabilizes **[O]**.

### Gaps
- No study tested whether women benefit from different RIR targets than men. Refalo 2024 included only 6 women.
- The Robinson 2024 quantitative slope (e.g., % hypertrophy per RIR) could not be extracted.

## 6. Rest intervals and time-saving set structures (supersets, drop sets, rest-pause, myo-reps)

### Takeaway
Rest ≥60 s, with roughly 90 s or more best for hypertrophy. Beyond ~90 s there is no clear additional benefit, though longer rest on heavy compounds preserves volume load. Antagonist or non-competing supersets cut session time by about a third with no measurable loss of hypertrophy. Drop sets produce similar hypertrophy to straight sets when volume is matched and take far less time. The data on rest-pause and myo-reps are thin.

### Cited Findings
- **Singer et al. 2024** (Front Sports Act Living, Bayesian meta-analysis). There is a small hypertrophy benefit for inter-set rest >60 s, possibly driven by the lower volume load at short rests, and no clear difference beyond ~90 s. Arm and thigh estimates leaned toward longer rest, all credible intervals crossed zero, and heterogeneity was substantial. NSCA guidance (30–90 s) is noted as conventional. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789/text)
- **Schoenfeld et al. 2016** trial in trained men: longer rests produced significantly greater 1RM and muscle thickness gains than short rests. — [Schoenfeld blog on the study](https://www.lookgreatnaked.com/blog/what-is-the-ideal-rest-interval-for-muscle-growth-implications-from-our-recent-study)
- **Zhang et al. 2025** (Sports Med; 19 studies, 313 participants), superset vs traditional sets. Chronic hypertrophy was similar (SMD −0.05, p = 0.87), as were maximal strength (SMD 0.10, p = 0.36) and strength endurance (SMD 0.07). Training efficiency was higher (SMD 1.74, p = 0.01). Agonist–antagonist pairs allowed more reps (SMD 0.68). Lactate was higher during (SMD 0.94) and after (SMD 1.13) sessions. A secondary source reports ~37% shorter sessions. — [Leeds Beckett eprint](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/11908); [Lyfta (secondary)](https://lyfta.app/articles/supersets-hypertrophy)
- **Sødal et al. 2023** (Sports Med Open) drop-set meta-analysis (5 studies in quantitative analysis). There was no significant hypertrophy difference vs traditional sets with volume matched. Secondary sources say drop-set protocols took about ½–⅓ of the time. — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/21e0b210caf1433594359186eee617b9)
- A 2026 drop-set meta-analysis (12 studies, 274 participants) found drop sets raise perceived exertion and blood lactate. — [Springer, Sports Med Open 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-026-01012-1)
- A 2026 meta-analysis (Tsartsapakis et al.) reportedly found a modest hypertrophy edge for rest-pause. Unverified, blog-only. — [Planfit (secondary)](https://planfit.ai/en/blog/drop-sets-for-hypertrophy)
- **Krzysztofik et al. 2019** systematic review (30 articles). Agonist–antagonist and upper–lower supersets, drop sets, cluster sets and BFR "may offer an additional stimulus or time advantage". There is a "lack of consensus", and specific guidelines are difficult to give. — [PubMed 31817252](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31817252/)

### Inferences
- **Rest defaults [B]**:
  - Heavy compounds: 2–3 min (min 90 s).
  - Machine compounds: 90–120 s.
  - Isolation: 60–90 s (min 60 s). The slightly shorter end may suit women given fatigue-resistance data [C].
- Allow "rest until ready" with a floor of 60 s and a ceiling of ~3–4 min.
- **Supersets [A/B]**: allowed whenever session time is limited. Pair non-competing muscles: push+pull, upper+lower, or quad/glute + upper. Do not pair two exercises that load the same muscle or grip, or two heavy axial compounds. Use 60–90 s between alternating exercises.
- **Drop sets / myo-reps / rest-pause [B for drop sets, C/O for others]**: use only on isolation or machine exercises, only on the final set, and at most 1–2 per muscle per session.
  - Count a set plus 1–2 drops as ~1.5–2 sets in the ledger, and a myo-rep cluster (activation set + 3–5 mini-sets) as ~2–3 sets. These counting factors are [O] convention, not evidence.
  - Prefer them in short sessions. Avoid them on barbell compounds.

### Gaps
- Weakley et al. 2017 (acute superset/tri-set responses) was not retrieved separately.
- Myo-reps specifically were not found in any controlled trial in the searched sources.
- The specific protocol of Schoenfeld 2016 (commonly cited as 3 min vs 1 min rest) was not verified this session.

## 7. Exercise selection: long muscle lengths, free weights vs machines, glutes, regional priorities

### Takeaway
Choose exercises that load muscles at long lengths: seated over lying leg curls, overhead triceps work, deep squats and lunges. Full ROM is the default, and lengthened partials are a promising but unproven add-on. Free weights and machines build similar muscle. For glutes, deep squats/lunges and hip thrusts both work, squats also grow quads and adductors, and EMG does not predict growth. Balance glute priority with adequate back and shoulder volume.

### Cited Findings
- **Maeo et al. 2021** (MSSE 53(4):825–837; 20 young adults, 12 weeks, within-subject). Seated leg curl beat prone leg curl for whole-hamstring volume (+14% vs +9%). The difference was in the biarticular heads; the short head was similar (+10% vs +9%). — [PubMed 33009197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/)
- **Maeo et al. 2022/2023** (Eur J Sport Sci). Overhead cable triceps extension grew the triceps more than neutral-arm extension, especially the long head (+28.5% vs +19.6%), despite lower loads. — [PubMed 35819335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/)
- **Pedrosa et al. 2022** (Eur J Sport Sci) in 45 untrained *women*. Partial-ROM knee extension in the initial, long-muscle-length range produced greater relative hypertrophy in some (distal) quadriceps regions than other ROM configurations, with no meaningful strength difference. — [ResearchGate](https://researchgate.net/profile/Gustavo-Pedrosa/publication/351538891_Partial_range_of_motion_training_elicits_favorable_improvements_in_muscular_adaptations_when_carried_out_at_long_muscle_lengths/links/61f1e6bbc5e3103375c2e9f9/Partial-range-of-motion-training-elicits-favorable-improvements-in-muscular-adaptations-when-carried-out-at-long-muscle-lengths.pdf)
- **Wolf et al. 2023** (IJSC, Bayesian meta-analysis). Full ROM was trivially better than partial ROM (SMD 0.12, 95% CI −0.02 to 0.26). A subgroup suggested a possible benefit of partials at long muscle lengths (−0.28, 95% CI −0.81 to 0.16, crossing zero). — [IUSCA journal](https://journal.iusca.org/index.php/Journal/article/view/182)
- **Haugen et al. 2023** (BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil; 13 studies, 1,016 participants). Free weight vs machine hypertrophy did not differ (SMD −0.055, CI −0.397 to 0.287, p = 0.751). Strength gains follow specificity: free-weight tests favor free-weight training. — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/a7282dba5e674176bde2a8239832d2f7); [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/free-weights-vs-machines/)
- **ACSM 2026**: equipment type and exercise complexity did not consistently affect outcomes. — [Newswise/ACSM](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years)
- **Plotkin et al. 2023** (Front Physiol; untrained adults, hip thrust n = 18 vs back squat n = 16, 9 weeks, MRI).
  - Gluteal growth was similar; point estimates slightly favored the hip thrust, with wide CIs.
  - The squat produced more quadriceps (+3.6 cm², CI 0.7–6.4) and adductor (+2.5 cm², CI 1.2–3.9) growth.
  - The hip thrust had higher glute EMG, which did not predict hypertrophy.
  - Deadlift transfer was similar.
  — [PubMed 37877099](https://www.pubmed.gov/37877099/); [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)
- **Barbalho et al. 2020** (Int J Sports Med 41(5):306–310; 22 *well-trained women*, 12 weeks). Back squat produced more gluteus maximus thickness gain (9.4% vs 3.7%) and quadriceps gain (12.2% vs 2%) than hip thrust. Hip thrust 1RM gains were similar between groups. — [Thieme](https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/abstract/10.1055/a-1082-1126)
- One proposed explanation for Barbalho is the larger hip-extension ROM in full-depth squats compared with the hip thrust's roughly 40–50° arc. — [Sci-Sport (secondary)](https://sci-sport.com/squat-vs-hip-thrust-force-et-hypertrophie-musculaires/)
- Practitioners dispute how far Barbalho generalizes. Barbell Medicine's Feigenbaum expects similar glute hypertrophy from both lifts, with large individual variation. — [Barbell Medicine forum](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/did-barbalho-2020-change-your-opinion-on-squats-vs-hip-thrust-for-glute-hypertrophy/8948)
- **Relevance for a female trainee**: the sex gap in absolute gains is upper-body only (Refalo 2025), and relative upper-body strength gains favor women (Roberts 2020). — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/dbf856c9d64840b59318c009c4abd626); [PubMed 32218059](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32218059/)

### Inferences
- **Rule: at least one lengthened-bias exercise per muscle per week [B/C]**:
  - Hamstrings: seated leg curl, RDL.
  - Triceps: overhead extension.
  - Quads: deep squat, split squat, leg extension (full ROM or lengthened partials).
  - Glutes: deep squat, lunge, split squat, step-up.
  - Plausible extensions (not tested in the retrieved sources; [O]): chest flyes or deep dumbbell press; incline dumbbell curls for biceps; standing calf raise with a deep stretch.
- **Lengthened partials**: optional on machines and isolation work, as the last 1–2 sets or after full-ROM failure. Never replace full ROM entirely **[C]**.
- **Glute block (inference from Plotkin + Barbalho)**: weekly glute work = 1 deep knee-dominant pattern (squat, leg press, split squat, lunge) + 1 hip-extension pattern (hip thrust or glute bridge) + 1 hinge (RDL, 45° back extension). Optionally add hip abduction for glute medius **[O]**. Hip thrusts are fine but should not be the only glute exercise.
- **Free weights vs machines**: treat as interchangeable for hypertrophy **[A/B]**. Choose by equipment, comfort and ability to train near failure safely. Machines suit 0–1 RIR sets; free weights suit any strength-specific goals.
- **Balance rules [O]**:
  - Weekly pull sets ≥ push sets; vertical and horizontal pulls both present.
  - Lateral delt direct work ≥6 sets/week when shoulders are a priority.
  - Upper-body fractional volume must not fall below ~10 sets for back, ~8 for chest/shoulders even when glutes are prioritized.
  - Common aesthetic priorities for women (glutes, back, shoulders) are a user preference, not a physiological need. Let the user pick 1–2 priority muscle groups.
- **Unilateral work**: include ≥1 unilateral lower-body exercise per week (split squat, lunge, step-up) for glute/quad length bias and side-to-side balance **[O]**.

### Gaps
- Contreras's hip thrust studies (e.g., the 2017 adolescent trial) were not retrieved.
- The 2024 Varovic meta-analysis on long vs short muscle lengths (k = 12) was reportedly trivial-effect but not verified.
- Unilateral vs bilateral hypertrophy evidence was not retrieved.
- No studies were found on glute medius/minimus hypertrophy from abduction work.
- Kassiano/Pedrosa calf and elbow-flexor lengthened-partial studies exist (2023 JSCR, 2025 PeerJ) but were not opened.

## 8. Progression, autoregulation and deloads

### Takeaway
Adding reps at a fixed load and adding load at fixed reps work about equally well. This supports double progression, which suits women's small load increments. RIR-based autoregulation is workable if calibrated against her logged accuracy. Deloads are opinion-based. Practitioners use about 1 week every ~4–8 weeks, cutting sets and effort while keeping frequency and exercises.

### Cited Findings
- **Plotkin et al. 2022** (PeerJ 10:e14142; 43 participants with ≥1 year lower-body training, 8 weeks). LOAD progression (fixed reps) vs REPS progression (fixed load) produced similar outcomes. Rectus femoris growth modestly favored REPS, and strength slightly favored LOAD (+2.0 kg [−2.4, 7.8]). "Both progressions of repetitions and load appear to be viable strategies." The start was 8–12 reps. — [PubMed 36199287](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36199287/); [PeerJ](https://peerj.com/articles/14142)
- **Bell, Strafford, Coleman, Androulakis Korakakis & Nolan 2023** (Sports Med Open; 3-round Delphi with strength/physique coaches). It defined deloading as "a period of reduced training stress designed to mitigate physiological and psychological fatigue, promote recovery, and enhance preparedness for subsequent training". Specific numeric consensus items could not be retrieved. — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/599784d71dff41b197ff5cf66a3d11dd)
- **Rogerson, Bell et al. 2024** (Sports Med Open; survey of 246 strength/physique athletes, 65 female):
  - Deloads lasted 6.4 ± 1.7 days, every 5.6 ± 2.3 weeks.
  - Reps per set and sets per week were reduced; frequency was unchanged; load decreased and RIR increased; exercise selection was maintained.
  - Every respondent deloaded, mainly for energy and fatigue management.
  — [PubMed 38499934](https://www.pubmed.gov/38499934/); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/7d15e260e0ac4cff9e8c685dd7335389)
- **Coach interview study (Bell et al., SportRxiv)**: deloads typically lasted 5–7 days, every 4–6 weeks, with "highly variable" periodicity. Volume and effort were the most-adjusted variables. — [SportRxiv 208](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/208)
- **Coleman et al. 2024** (PeerJ) tested a 1-week deload at the midpoint of a 9-week program. It is a single trial, and its outcome details were not retrieved. — [PeerJ 16777](https://peerj.com/articles/16777)
- Halperin 2022: RIR underprediction is about 0.95 reps, and accuracy is unaffected by training status. — [BVS](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/ses/resource/pt/mdl-34542869)

### Inferences
- **Double progression (default) [B]**: each exercise has a rep range (e.g., 8–12). Hold the load until **all** working sets reach the top of the range at ≥ target RIR, then add the smallest available increment and return to the bottom of the range.
  - Upper-body increments: ~2.5–5%.
  - Lower-body increments: ~5–10%.
  - If the next increment is >10% of the load, extend the range (e.g., to 15–20 reps) or add a set before jumping, rather than jumping load.
- **Load autoregulation within a session [O]**: if logged reps exceed the range top by ≥2 on set 1, increase load for set 2. If reps fall below the range bottom, reduce load ~5–10%.
- **Deload [O]**: planned every 5–8 weeks of hard training (≈1 per mesocycle) for 5–7 days.
  - Sets −40–50%, RIR ≥3–4, loads equal or −10%; keep frequency and exercises.
  - If she trains ≤2×/week, lengthen the interval to ~8–10 weeks [O].
  - Allow an early reactive deload (see plateau rules).

### Gaps
- No RCT evidence was found on optimal deload frequency. The Delphi's numeric principles could not be extracted (PMC blocked).
- Coleman 2024 results (whether the deload impaired or helped hypertrophy or strength) were not retrieved.
- No trial compares autoregulated vs fixed progression specifically in women.

## 9. Concurrent training interference (cardio, HIIT, plyometrics)

### Takeaway
Moderate aerobic training does not measurably reduce whole-muscle hypertrophy (Schumann 2022). Explosive strength suffers, especially when aerobic and strength training share a session. Fiber-level data hint at a small cost, and interference rises with running, frequency and duration. Default: up to ~2–3 cardio sessions per week of ≤30–40 min, prefer cycling and low-impact options, and separate cardio from lifting by ≥3 h or do it after lifting.

### Cited Findings
- **Schumann et al. 2022** (Sports Med; 43 studies). Concurrent aerobic plus strength training had no meaningful effect on whole-muscle hypertrophy vs strength-only (SMD −0.01, 95% CI −0.16 to 0.18) or on maximal strength. Explosive strength was attenuated (SMD −0.28), more so when both were done in the same session than when separated by ≥3 h. Results were independent of aerobic modality, frequency, training status and age. — [PMC9474354](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9474354/)
- **Lundberg, Schumann et al. 2022** fiber-level meta-analysis. Concurrent training "may have a small negative effect on fiber hypertrophy". Preliminary data suggest more interference when the aerobic work is running rather than cycling, at least for type I fibers. — [SportRxiv preprint record (mapping per search summary)](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/37)
- **Wilson et al. 2012** (JSCR; 21 studies, 422 effect sizes).
  - Running, but not cycling, significantly reduced hypertrophy and strength gains.
  - Endurance frequency and duration correlated negatively with hypertrophy, strength and power (r ≈ −0.26 to −0.75).
  - Mean hypertrophy ES: strength-only 1.23, concurrent 0.85, endurance-only 0.27.
  — [BVS/MEDLINE 22002517](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/sms/resource/pt/mdl-22002517); [Biolayne (secondary)](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-8/concurrent-training-cardio-will-probably-not-kill-your-gains/)

### Inferences
- **Cardio budget while building muscle [B/O]**:
  - Steady-state: ≤3 sessions/week, 20–40 min, Zone 2 (conversational).
  - HIIT: ≤2 sessions/week, ≤20 min of work+recovery; prefer bike, rower or incline walk over running when lower-body hypertrophy is a priority (Wilson, Lundberg).
  - Total hard cardio ≤ ~90–120 min/week unless she prioritizes endurance [O].
- **Sequencing [B/O]**: separate cardio and lifting by ≥3 h when possible (Schumann's subgroup), ideally on different days. If done in the same session, lift first and keep post-lift cardio low to moderate. No hard leg HIIT or long runs within ~24 h before a lower-body/glute session [O].
- **Plyometrics/jumps**: low volume (e.g., 2–4 sets × 3–6 reps, ≤40–60 contacts/session), placed at the *start* of a session after warm-up and before heavy lifting. Not counted toward hypertrophy volume [O].
- Daily steps and walking count as low-interference activity and need not be limited [O].

### Gaps
- No study gives exact "safe" cardio minutes per week for hypertrophy in women. The numbers above are inferences from Wilson's negative frequency and duration correlations plus Schumann's null whole-muscle result.
- HIIT-specific and plyometric-specific interference meta-analyses were not retrieved.
- Secondary claims of a "6-hour gap" requirement were not supported by primary abstracts.

## 10. Nutrition levers relevant to programming

### Takeaway
Protein 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day; the Morton 2018 breakpoint is 1.62 g/kg with an upper CI of 2.2. Creatine monohydrate is effective and safe in premenopausal women; 3–5 g/day is the standard maintenance dose but was not verified in a primary source here. Eat at maintenance or a small surplus: larger surpluses mainly add fat without more muscle. Avoid prescribing a calorie deficit when the goal is building muscle.

### Cited Findings
- **Morton et al. 2018** (BJSM 52:376–384; 49 trials, 1,863 participants). Protein supplementation increased fat-free mass (+0.30 kg) and 1RM. FFM gains plateaued at 1.62 g/kg/day (95% CI 1.03–2.20). Effects were larger in resistance-trained participants and smaller with age. — [BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/52/6/376)
- Stronger By Science notes that Morton's authors were not confident a true breakpoint exists, and that their data suggest any breakpoint is probably *higher* than 1.62 g/kg. — [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=61316)
- **Smith-Ryan et al. 2021** (Nutrients, "Creatine Supplementation in Women's Health: A Lifespan Perspective"). Creatine "appears to be effective for improving strength and exercise performance in pre-menopausal women". A systematic review cited there found no adverse effects on GI, renal, hepatic or cardiovascular systems in women. High doses (0.3 g/kg/day) have been studied post-menopause, and the authors call for dose-optimization research. — [PMC7998865](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7998865/)
- A 3–5 g/day protocol for women appears in secondary sources but was not confirmed in the primary review text. — [creatine.pages.dev (secondary, unverified)](https://creatine.pages.dev/en/research/smith-ryan-2021-creatine-women)
- **Helms et al. 2023** (Sports Med Open; trained lifters, 3×/week, 8 weeks; maintenance vs +5% vs +15% energy; 17 completers). No group differences in muscle thickness or squat 1RM; bench 1RM gains were greater with the +15% surplus. Skinfold gains were greater in the high-surplus group. Body-mass change strongly predicted skinfold change. Faster gaining "primarily increase[s] fat gain". — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/ab6ce09af2cc4daa9a1c2e9b88e1b1e9)

### Inferences
- **Protein target**: 1.6 g/kg/day minimum, 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day target range **[A/B]**. For a 60 kg woman that is about 96–132 g/day.
- **Distribution [O]**: 3–5 protein feedings of ~0.3–0.4 g/kg (≈20–40 g), including one within a few hours of training.
- **Creatine**: 3–5 g/day creatine monohydrate, no loading needed, taken daily regardless of training day **[B for efficacy in women; O for exact dose]**.
- **Energy [B/O]**: maintenance (recomposition) for beginners or anyone with higher body fat; a small surplus (≈+5–10%, i.e., slow weight gain) for intermediates. Do not run a deficit in a muscle-building phase. If bodyweight is flat and multiple lifts stall for ≥4 weeks, prompt a nutrition check (section 11).
- Low energy availability is a risk specific to women, but evidence on it was not retrieved here. The generator should not encourage under-eating or excessive cardio [O].

### Gaps
- Schoenfeld & Aragon 2018 (per-meal protein distribution, ~0.4 g/kg × 4) was not retrieved; the distribution guidance is unverified.
- Body-recomposition evidence specifically in trained or untrained women was not retrieved. Barakat et al. 2020 is commonly cited but was not opened.
- Low energy availability/RED-S thresholds were not retrieved.
- Primary creatine dosing data for young women were not verified.

## 11. Beginner vs intermediate needs; detecting and responding to plateaus

### Takeaway
There are almost no direct trials on how beginner and intermediate women should be programmed differently, or on plateau-detection algorithms. Rules here are mostly inference from dose-response curves and coaching convention. Beginners grow on lower volume, need more technique buffer, and can progress load almost every session. Intermediates need more volume, closer-to-failure isolation work and planned deloads. A plateau should be flagged from logged rep/load or e1RM trends over 3 exposures.

### Cited Findings
- **Pelland 2025**: diminishing returns of volume, i.e., the first sets yield the largest per-set gains. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398333408_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_Response_Meta-Regressions_Exploring_the_Effects_of_Weekly_Volume_and_Frequency_on_Muscle_Hypertrophy_and_Strength_Gains_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_ResponseJ_C_Pelland_et_al)
- **ACSM 2026**: start with all major muscles ≥2 days/week and build gradually. — [Newswise/ACSM](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years)
- Roberts 2020's female upper-body strength advantage came mostly from untrained samples, so early strength jumps in women can be large. — [PubMed 32218059](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32218059/)
- Morton 2018: the protein effect on FFM was larger in trained individuals. — [BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/52/6/376)
- Halperin 2022: training status did not moderate RIR prediction accuracy, so beginners are not demonstrably worse at RIR estimation. — [BVS](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/ses/resource/pt/mdl-34542869)
- Refalo 2025: training experience did not alter the sex comparison of hypertrophy. — [Napier](https://www.napier.ac.uk/research-and-innovation/research-search/outputs/sex-differences-in-absolute-and-relative-changes-in-muscle-size-following-resistance)

### Inferences
- **Classification [O]**: *beginner* = <6–12 months of consistent (≥2×/week) training, or still adding load/reps nearly every session. *Intermediate* = ≥1 year, or progress now needs weeks rather than sessions.
- **Beginner settings [O/B]**:
  - 8–12 fractional sets/muscle/week, 2–3 full-body sessions.
  - Compounds at 2–3 RIR, isolation at 1–2.
  - Mostly stable machines plus 3–4 key free-weight patterns.
  - Double progression every session.
  - Deload only when needed (reactive) or every ~8 weeks.
- **Intermediate settings [O/B]**:
  - 12–20 fractional sets/muscle/week (priority muscles toward 16–20+).
  - Compounds at 1–2 RIR, isolation at 0–1.
  - Mesocycles of 4–6 hard weeks plus a deload, with volume ramps of +1–2 sets/muscle per week inside the block.
  - Rotate 1–2 exercise variants per muscle per mesocycle.
- **Plateau detection rule (exercise level) [O]**: compute e1RM, e.g., Epley: load × (1 + reps/30), using reps + logged RIR as reps-to-failure. A plateau = no e1RM increase across **3 consecutive exposures** (or **≥3 weeks**) at the same load and rep target, with RIR at or below target (i.e., she is trying hard).
- **Plateau response ladder [O]**:
  1. If session adherence is <80% of planned sets, or sleep, stress or nutrition flags are set, fix those first; no program change.
  2. If weekly fractional sets for that muscle are below the user's ceiling (e.g., <20) and fatigue ratings are fine, add +1–2 sets/week to that muscle.
  3. If volume is already high, or fatigue/soreness ratings are high, or performance is *declining* (≥5% e1RM drop or −2 reps at the same load in 2 consecutive sessions), trigger a reactive deload, then resume at ~80% of the prior volume.
  4. If the same exercise plateaus twice in a row across mesocycles, swap to a variant with the same movement pattern, preferably one with more lengthened bias.
  5. If ≥50% of tracked lifts plateau together and bodyweight trend is flat or falling for ≥4 weeks, prompt a nutrition check: protein ≥1.6 g/kg, move from maintenance to a small surplus.

### Gaps
- No empirical validation of any plateau-detection threshold (2 vs 3 sessions, e1RM vs reps) was found. These rules are coaching convention.
- No trial compared beginner vs intermediate women on different volumes.

### Gaps
- Several [O] values are defaults, not evidence: deload interval, plateau thresholds, cardio minute caps, drop-set counting factors, the 48 h same-muscle gap and session time heuristics. The generator should expose them as tunable and adapt them from her logged response.
- No female-specific RCTs exist for volume, RIR or rest prescription. If her logs show good recovery at high set counts, raising the volume ceiling (e.g., toward 24 fractional sets for priority muscles) is plausible but unproven.
- Full texts of Pelland 2025, the Bell 2023 Delphi, Coleman 2024, Lundberg 2022 and Smith-Ryan 2021 could not be opened (network-blocked hosts: sportrxiv.org, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, peerj.com). Specific numeric values from those papers rely on abstracts and secondary summaries.
