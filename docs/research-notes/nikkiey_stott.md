## 2. Nikkiey Stott (and, briefly, Dr Nicky Keay): background, audience, recommendations, hormones

### Takeaway
Stott is a registered nurse (BSN), certified personal trainer and former natural pro bodybuilder who co-founded WarriorBabe in 2018. She sells macro-tracking plus strength-training coaching, aimed overwhelmingly at women 40+ in perimenopause and menopause. Her core message is to lift 3–4 times a week with progressive overload, eat enough (especially protein), track macros, use cardio as a tool rather than the main strategy, walk daily, and reverse-diet out of chronic under-eating. No source retrieved gives her rep ranges or her stance on training to failure.

## 3. Grading her main claims against the evidence

### Takeaway
Her training basics are well supported: lift 3–4 times a week, progressively overload, stay on a program, and don't rely on cardio. The nutrition basics are also sound: enough protein, avoid chronic under-eating. Her metabolism framing is weak: "muscle burns calories 24/7" is true in direction but trivial in size, and reverse dieting has no controlled-trial support for "fixing" metabolism. Any menstrual-phase programming she or Keay endorses is weak. Keay's core claim, that lost or irregular periods signal under-fuelling, is strongly supported.

### Cited Findings
| Claim (who) | Grade | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Women should prioritise strength training to build muscle and change body composition (Stott) | **Strong** | ACSM 2026 position stand (137 systematic reviews, >30,000 participants): hypertrophy is enhanced by ≥10 sets/week per muscle. Women gain muscle similarly to men in relative terms. — [Newswise/ACSM](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years); [Roberts et al. 2020, PubMed 32218059](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32218059/); [Refalo et al. 2025, PeerJ (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/dbf856c9d64840b59318c009c4abd626) |
| 3–4 sessions per week, 45–60 min, full-body to start (Stott) | **Strong to moderate** (adequate dose) | ACSM 2026: train all major muscles ≥2 days/week. Pelland 2025: frequency itself has weak or negligible effects on hypertrophy once volume is equated, so 3–4 days is enough if weekly sets are adequate. — [Newswise/ACSM](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years); [Pelland et al. 2025 (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398333408_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_Response_Meta-Regressions_Exploring_the_Effects_of_Weekly_Volume_and_Frequency_on_Muscle_Hypertrophy_and_Strength_Gains_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_ResponseJ_C_Pelland_et_al) |
| Progressive overload is essential (Stott) | **Strong** (as a principle) | Progressive increases in load or volume are central to ACSM guidance, and more weekly volume yields more growth with diminishing returns. — [Newswise/ACSM](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years); [Pelland 2025](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398333408_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_Response_Meta-Regressions_Exploring_the_Effects_of_Weekly_Volume_and_Frequency_on_Muscle_Hypertrophy_and_Strength_Gains_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_ResponseJ_C_Pelland_et_al) |
| "Changing workouts every 2 weeks is killing your results" (Stott) | **Moderate** for "stay consistent so you can track progression". **Opinion** for the specific 2-week harm. | ACSM 2026 found that periodization and set-structure variations "did not consistently impact training outcomes". Constant program changes mainly make overload hard to track. No trial shows that a 2-week rotation causes harm. — [Newswise/ACSM](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years) |
| Cardio is a tool, not the main strategy. Cardio while eating less makes fat loss harder (Stott) | **Moderate** that lifting plus diet beats cardio-centric dieting for body composition. **Strong evidence against** any implied idea that cardio hurts muscle gain. | Concurrent aerobic plus strength training did not reduce whole-muscle hypertrophy (SMD −0.01) or maximal strength. Explosive strength was blunted more when both were done in the same session. — [Schumann et al. 2022, PMC9474354](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9474354/) |
| "Muscle burns calories 24/7; cardio only while you do it" (Stott) | **Weak / misleading in size** | Skeletal muscle's resting rate is about 13 kcal/kg/day (Elia), confirmed by Wang et al. 2010 (about 12.6 in adults over 50). Adding 2 kg of muscle raises resting expenditure by only about 25 kcal/day. — [Wang et al. 2010, AJCN, PubMed 20962155](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20962155/); [PMC2883012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2883012) |
| Nutrition first for fat loss (Stott) | **Strong** for fat loss (energy balance) | Not directly relevant to a muscle-gain goal. For hypertrophy, eating at maintenance or in a small surplus matters more (next rows). — [Helms et al. 2023 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/ab6ce09af2cc4daa9a1c2e9b88e1b1e9) |
| High protein (≈30–35% of calories; unofficially ≈1 g/lb) (Stott) | **Strong** for ≥1.6 g/kg/day. ~2.2 g/kg (≈1 g/lb) is a safe upper target, not a requirement. | Morton 2018 (49 RCTs): gains in fat-free mass plateaued at about 1.62 g/kg/day (95% CI 1.03–2.20). — [Morton et al. 2018, BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/52/6/376) |

Additional evidence relevant to what Stott does not say (rep ranges and failure):
- Training to failure adds little over stopping 1–2 reps short. In Refalo 2023 the effect was trivial (ES 0.19), with no advantage when failure was strictly defined. In Refalo 2024 (12 men and 6 women), quadriceps growth was identical but fatigue was greater with failure. ACSM 2026 found that training to failure "did not consistently impact" outcomes. — [Refalo 2023, PubMed 36334240](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334240/); [Refalo 2024, PubMed 38393985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38393985/); [Newswise/ACSM](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years)

### Inferences
- Her training claims score well because they are the uncontroversial core of resistance-training science. Her weaker claims all sit on the metabolism-marketing side, and they matter more for midlife fat loss than for a 22-year-old gaining muscle.
- The "muscle burns calories 24/7" line is a common fitness-industry overstatement. The real body-composition benefit of lifting comes from adding and keeping lean mass while dieting, not from a large rise in resting metabolic rate.

## 4. Is her approach suitable for a healthy 22-year-old woman whose main goal is lean muscle?

### Takeaway
Her training template partly suits a 22-year-old, but the program as a whole does not. The lifting principles transfer well (3–4 sessions a week, progressive overload, consistency, protein, not over-relying on cardio). The package is built for women 40+ who are trying to lose fat after years of under-eating. Its menopause macro splits, reverse-dieting and fat-loss framing, and price tag are poor fits. She should take the training principles and protein emphasis, and add the missing specifics (weekly sets, RIR) from the evidence.
