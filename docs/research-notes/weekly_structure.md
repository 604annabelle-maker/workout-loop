# Weekly training structure for a 22-year-old intermediate woman: 4 lifting days (~60 min) + 2 group classes

Evidence grades used throughout: **[Strong]** = consistent meta-analytic or position-stand evidence; **[Moderate]** = meta-analytic but limited, heterogeneous or partly conflicting; **[Weak]** = single studies, preprints, small samples or secondary reporting only; **[Opinion]** = coach or author opinion, or my own programming inference.

Set-counting convention used in all set math below (taken from Pelland et al.'s best-fitting "fractional" method): a set counts as **1.0** for a muscle that is a prime mover and **0.5** for a synergist (e.g., triceps and front delts on bench; biceps and rear delts on rows; hamstrings and quads on deadlifts). Conditioning finishers (swings, sled, bike sprints) are **not** counted.

## Q1. Can a 4-day "2 muscle groups per session" split still give every major muscle ≥2 sessions/week and ~10–20 hard sets/week? What do Schoenfeld 2016/2019, Grgic 2018 and Pelland 2025 say about once vs twice weekly at equal volume? Is a once-a-week body-part split meaningfully worse?

### Takeaway
Yes, but only if the two "headline" muscles rotate across an upper/lower axis (e.g., Glutes+Hams / Back+Chest / Shoulders+Back / Quads+Glutes), counting synergist work at 0.5 and using a short antagonist or arms superset as the finisher. A strict four-body-part split (Glutes+Hams / Back+Biceps / Quads+Calves / Chest+Shoulders+Triceps) trains glutes twice but most upper-body muscles only once. When weekly sets are equal, once-weekly training is **not meaningfully worse for muscle growth [Moderate]**. It is **probably somewhat worse for "lifting heavier" [Moderate, conflicting]**, and in practice it caps productive weekly sets for each muscle at about what one session can usefully hold, around 10–11 [Weak].

### Cited Findings
**Frequency, hypertrophy**
- Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2016 (*Sports Medicine* 46(11):1689–1697), 10 studies: higher frequency gave a larger hypertrophy effect size than lower frequency (0.49 ± 0.08 vs 0.30 ± 0.07; P = 0.002). The authors concluded that major muscles should be trained at least twice weekly. [Moderate at the time, now superseded] — [Schoenfeld 2016 PDF](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s40279-016-0543-8.pdf); summary: [Look Great Naked blog](https://lookgreatnaked.com/blog/how-many-times-should-you-train-a-muscle-each-week)
- Critique of the 2016 analysis: many included studies did not equate total sets or work, and higher-frequency groups often did more weekly sets, so frequency and volume were confounded — [Menno Henselmans review](https://mennohenselmans.com/training-frequency-2018-meta-analysis-review/)
- Schoenfeld, Grgic & Krieger 2019 (*Journal of Sports Sciences*) re-ran the analysis with 25 studies. When weekly volume was equated, frequency had **no significant effect** on hypertrophy. Higher frequencies grew more muscle only in studies where volume was not equated, i.e., through extra volume. Practical reading: for a fixed weekly volume, frequency can be chosen by preference. [Moderate] — [Menno Henselmans review](https://mennohenselmans.com/training-frequency-2018-meta-analysis-review/); [GetFitCraft summary](https://getfitcraft.com/science/training-frequency-research). (I could not open the primary 2019 paper; see Gaps.)
- Narrative review of frequency and hypertrophy (Grgic et al.): [ECU repository record](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworkspost2013/5665)

**Frequency, strength**
- Grgic et al. 2018 (*Sports Medicine*): overall, higher frequency was associated with larger strength gains, but the effect was small (d = 0.265; 95% CI 0.08–0.45; p = 0.0088). In **volume-equated** studies there was **no significant frequency effect** (p = 0.421). The authors framed higher frequency as a tool for adding weekly volume. A sex subgroup analysis found a significant frequency effect in **women** (p = 0.030) but not in men (p = 0.190). [Moderate] — [PubMed 29470825](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29470825)
- Saric et al. 2018: in volume-equated trained men, 3 vs 6 sessions/week gave similar muscular adaptations — [ResearchGate PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Jozo-Grgic/publication/327435932_Resistance_Training_Frequencies_of_3_and_6_Times_Per_Week_Produce_Similar_Muscular_Adaptations_in_Resistance-Trained_Men/links/5bd26d6892851c6b278f311d/Resistance-Training-Frequencies-of-3-and-6-Times-Per-Week-Produce-Similar-Muscular-Adaptations-in-Resistance-Trained-Men.pdf)

**Pelland et al. 2025 dose-response meta-regressions (Sports Medicine; doi 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w)**
- 67 studies, about 2,058 participants. A citation lists online publication as 4 Dec 2025, while a blog dates it Feb 2026 (date conflict) — [sponet record](https://sponet.de/sponet/Record/4097139?lng=en); [BibBase record](https://bibbase.org/network/publication/joshuapelland-jacobremmert-zacrobinson-sethhinson-michaelzourdos-theresistancetrainingdoseresponsemetaregressionsexploringtheeectsofweeklyvolumeandfrequencyonmusclehypertrophyandstrengthgain-2024); [train.fitness summary](https://train.fitness/personal-trainer-blogs/not-all-sets-are-created-equal)
- **Volume:** more weekly sets increased both hypertrophy and strength, with diminishing returns that were **more pronounced for strength** than for hypertrophy. The "fractional" counting method (indirect set = 0.5) predicted outcomes best. [Strong] — [train.fitness summary](https://train.fitness/personal-trainer-blogs/not-all-sets-are-created-equal); [sponet record](https://sponet.de/sponet/Record/4097139?lng=en)
- **Frequency:** for **hypertrophy**, the posterior probability of a positive frequency effect was below 100%, which is compatible with a negligible effect. For **strength**, the probability was 100% (strength rises with frequency, with diminishing returns). [Moderate] — [train.fitness summary](https://train.fitness/personal-trainer-blogs/not-all-sets-are-created-equal)
- Companion per-session preprint (Remmert, Pelland, Robinson, Hinson, Zourdos 2025, SportRxiv, **not peer-reviewed**): the "point of undetectable outcome superiority" is about **11 fractional sets per muscle per session for hypertrophy** and about **2 direct sets per session for strength**. Both curves show diminishing returns, and data at very high per-session volumes are sparse. Press coverage says the authors suggest people chasing strength may do better by **raising frequency with shorter sessions**. [Weak] — [SportRxiv preprint 537](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537); [EurekAlert release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1086804); [FAU news](https://fau.edu/newsdesk/articles/build-muscle-gain-strength-study.php)
- Secondary coverage claims strength "often plateaus beyond about 3 fractional sets per week". This reads like a garbled per-session figure, so treat it as **unverified/suspect** — [Biolayne article](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-36/too-much-muscle-math-finding-the-ideal-training-volume-per-session/)

**Guidelines**
- ACSM 2026 Position Stand ("Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews", *MSSE* 58(3):512–545). It covers 137 systematic reviews and >30,000 participants and is the first update since 2009. Its central message is that any resistance training beats none and that adherence matters most — [Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1119943); [McMaster news](https://news.mcmaster.ca/?p=30899)
- ACSM 2026: train all major muscle groups **at least 2 days/week** [Strong as a guideline] — [ACSM infographic PDF](https://acsm.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Position-Stand-infographic.pdf); [Rep Fitness summary](https://repfitness.com/blogs/training/acsm-updated-resistance-training-guidelines)
- ACSM 2026 (secondary reporting): about **≥10 sets per muscle per week** for hypertrophy, with a dose-response beyond that — [NFPT](https://nfpt.com/beyond-the-guidelines-what-the-2026-acsm-position-stand-gets-right-and-what-it-changes-for-personal-trainers/); [Club Solutions](https://clubsolutionsmagazine.com/2026/04/acsm-has-a-new-position-stand-for-resistance-training-heres-what-you-need-to-know/)

**Sex-specific context**

No difference for lower-body strength. Most included participants were untrained. [Moderate] — [PubMed 32218059](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32218059/)
- Refalo et al. 2025 (*PeerJ*, Bayesian): **absolute** upper-body (not lower-body) hypertrophy favoured males. This concerns absolute, not relative, change — [PeerJ 19042](https://peerj.com/articles/19042)

### Inferences
**Comparison of the four structures she asked about** (my set math, fractional counting, 4 × 60 min, "few exercises, more sets"):

| Structure | Groups per session | Direct sessions/muscle/week | Realistic weekly fractional sets | Fit to her preferences | Main trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Strict pairing split (G+H / Back+Bi / Quads+Calves / Chest+Sh+Tri) | 2 (push day 3) | Glutes 2× (squats/lunges); quads, hams, back, chest, shoulders, arms 1× | Lower body 12–19; upper body ~8–12 | High | Upper body 1×/week; hams and chest <10 unless patched; bench/OHP practised once; push day has 3 groups |

| Hybrid 2 lower + 1 upper + 1 full-body | 2 (full-body day 3–4) | 2× everything | Lower 11–20; back/arms 7–11 | Moderate–high (she accepts "sometimes full body") | Arms and back at the low end; full-body day touches legs a 3rd time (lightly) |

- **Is once-a-week body-part training meaningfully worse?** For muscle, not if weekly sets match: the volume-equated analyses (Schoenfeld 2019; Pelland 2025 hypertrophy frequency effect compatible with negligible) agree. The practical catch is that a once-weekly session for chest, back or shoulders must hold all 10+ weekly sets. That is at or above the ~11-sets-per-session point where the Remmert preprint can no longer detect added benefit, and it takes most of a 60-minute session for one muscle. So a 1×/week split tends to cap usable volume at about 10–12 sets/week per muscle. For strength, Pelland's 100% probability of a positive frequency effect, the ACSM ≥2×/week advice, Grgic's significant frequency effect in women, and the "~2 productive strength sets per session" preprint figure all favour practising main lifts twice weekly. Grgic's volume-equated null result and Pelland's diminishing returns mean the edge is probably modest. **Net: a 1× split is a small, real disadvantage for "lift heavier" and for upper-body volume, not a dealbreaker for lean muscle.** [Moderate inference]
- Her own pairing split already trains glutes twice (deadlift/hip thrust day + squat/lunge day), which suits a glute-priority goal. The weak points are hamstrings, chest and arms at 1× and ~8–9 sets.
- Women showed equal or better **relative** strength and size gains (Roberts 2020), so nothing in the evidence calls for a different frequency structure for her than for men. Same principles apply. [Moderate inference]

## Q2. Combining strength ("lift heavier") with hypertrophy: heavy main lift then moderate-rep accessories, daily undulating periodization (DUP), and frequency of practice on main lifts

### Takeaway
Put one heavy compound "main lift" at the start of each session (3–6 reps at about ≥80% 1RM, 3–4 sets, RIR 1–3), then 2 supersets of moderate-rep accessories (6–15 reps, RIR 0–2). Practise each main movement pattern at least twice a week: once heavy, once as a lighter or moderate variation. This gives a built-in weekly undulation. Formal DUP is optional: it may give a small 1RM edge in trained lifters and makes no difference to hypertrophy.

### Cited Findings
- ACSM 2026 (secondary reporting): for strength, use **≥80% 1RM, 2–3 sets per exercise, full range of motion, ≥2×/week**. For hypertrophy, loads from **30–100% 1RM** produce similar growth if effort is high. Stopping at about **1–2 reps in reserve** gives results similar to training to failure when volume is matched. [Strong for load/effort, guideline-level for strength prescription] — [Rep Fitness summary](https://repfitness.com/blogs/training/acsm-updated-resistance-training-guidelines); [NFPT](https://nfpt.com/beyond-the-guidelines-what-the-2026-acsm-position-stand-gets-right-and-what-it-changes-for-personal-trainers/); [Club Solutions](https://clubsolutionsmagazine.com/2026/04/acsm-has-a-new-position-stand-for-resistance-training-heres-what-you-need-to-know/)
- Pelland 2025: strength shows **stronger diminishing returns to volume** than hypertrophy and a **positive frequency effect** (posterior probability 100%) [Moderate] — [train.fitness summary](https://train.fitness/personal-trainer-blogs/not-all-sets-are-created-equal)
- Remmert et al. 2025 preprint: about **2 direct sets per session** is the point beyond which added strength benefit is undetectable. Authors suggest more frequent, shorter exposures for strength [Weak, preprint] — [SportRxiv 537](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1086804)
- Moesgaard et al. 2022 (*Sports Medicine*): with volume equated, periodized training produced larger 1RM gains than non-periodized training **in trained but not untrained** participants, and periodization did not affect hypertrophy. Undulating periodization (DUP or weekly) beat linear for 1RM with a **trivial-to-small** effect. Examine also mentions a 2026 meta-analysis that did **not** confirm an undulating advantage (unverified). [Weak–Moderate, conflicting] — [Examine FAQ](https://examine.com/faq/are-periodized-training-programs-superior-for-strength-gains)
- Grgic et al. 2017 (*PeerJ*), linear vs DUP for hypertrophy, 13 studies: pooled ES −0.02 (95% CI −0.25 to 0.21; p = 0.848), i.e., **no difference** [Moderate] — [PeerJ 3695](https://peerj.com/articles/3695); [PMC5571788](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5571788/)
- Grgic 2018: a significant frequency effect on strength in women (p = 0.030), not in men (subgroup, not volume-equated) [Weak] — [PubMed 29470825](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29470825)

### Inferences
- **Session architecture (all templates):** (1) Main lift, 4 × 3–6, RIR 1–3, straight sets, 2.5–3 min rest. Antagonist pairing on upper days is fine, e.g., bench + row. (2) Superset A: 2 accessories, 3 × 6–12. (3) Superset B: 2 accessories, 3 × 10–20. (4) 5-min finisher. [Opinion/inference grounded in ACSM + Pelland]
- **Built-in weekly undulation:** each pattern gets a "heavy" exposure (3–6 reps) and a "moderate/volume" exposure (8–15 reps) on another day, e.g., bench heavy Tue → push-ups or incline Thu; OHP heavy Thu → incline press Tue; pull-up heavy Thu → pulldown Tue. This captures most of the frequency-for-strength benefit without adding muscle groups to a day. [Inference]
- **Lower-body main lifts** (squat, deadlift) are heavy only once each per week in the recommended template. To push lower-body strength further, add **2 top sets** of the other main lift on each lower day, e.g., 2 × 3 paused squat at RIR 3–4 after Monday's deadlift. The preprint suggests ~2 sets/session captures most of the per-session strength benefit, but this adds a third muscle to the day, so it is optional. [Inference, Weak evidence base]
- **Block rotation** [Opinion]: alternate 4–6-week blocks. In a strength emphasis, mains are 4 × 3–5 and accessories 3 sets. In a hypertrophy emphasis, mains are 3 × 6–8 and accessories +1 set. Progress the mains by load (double progression) and the accessories by reps then load.
- Women's larger relative upper-body strength gains (Roberts 2020) suggest bench, OHP and pull-ups will move quickly. Twice-weekly practice of those patterns is worth keeping. [Inference]

### Gaps
- Could not verify the exact Moesgaard 2022 effect sizes or the 2026 meta-analysis that Examine cites.
- No direct trial was found comparing "heavy main lift + moderate accessories within each session" vs DUP across sessions in women. Recommendations here are inference.

## Q3. Placing 2 group classes for recovery and interference; what to avoid before heavy leg days; how class type changes this

### Takeaway
Concurrent cardio or classes do **not** meaningfully blunt hypertrophy or maximal strength over time. The problem is acute: leg-heavy, intense classes (spin, HIIT, barre) reduce performance in the **same muscles** for many hours. Keep any leg-heavy class **≥48 h before a heavy squat/deadlift day** (24 h is the minimum). If it shares a day with lifting, put it **after** lifting, ideally **≥3 h later**, or on an upper-body day. Pilates barely interferes and can go anywhere.

### Cited Findings
- Schumann et al. 2022 (*Sports Medicine* 52:601–612): concurrent aerobic + strength training **did not compromise hypertrophy or maximal strength**, but **explosive strength gains may be attenuated, especially when both are done in the same session** [Moderate] — [PubMed 34757594](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/); [JCU full-text PDF](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/71546/1/Schumann2021_Article_CompatibilityOfConcurrentAerob.pdf)
- Schumann preprint: attenuation of explosive strength was significant with same-session concurrent training (p = 0.043) but not when sessions were separated by **≥3 h** (p > 0.05). Not confirmed in the published version [Weak] — [SportRxiv preprint 37](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/37)
- Sporer & Wenger 2003 (*JSCR* 17(4):638–644; 16 men): strength performance was impaired 4 h and 8 h after cycling intervals. The authors advised **~24 h** between aerobic and strength sessions when optimal strength performance matters. Impairment was **limited to the muscle groups used** in the aerobic work, and HIIT vs continuous cycling made no difference [Weak] — [Kravitz/UNM concurrent training article](https://www.unm.edu/~lkravitz/Article%20folder/concurrent.html); [IDEA Fit summary](https://www.ideafit.com/concurrent-training); [UVic thesis](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/bitstreams/8eb62a10-b8f0-4d6c-badf-5fcff0bace30/download)
- A later review concluded that 4–8 h recovery may be enough to avoid interference with **strength-endurance** performance, and that cycling had more negative acute effects on strength endurance than running [Weak] — [Kravitz/UNM article](https://www.unm.edu/~lkravitz/Article%20folder/concurrent.html)
- An aerobic bout before strength exercise impaired subsequent strength-endurance performance, with a **larger interference effect after cycling** than treadmill exercise [Weak] — [PMC4425969](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4425969)
- HIIT after leg extensions showed **no acute molecular interference** on protein signaling or mRNA (n = 10 men) [Weak] — [Frontiers in Physiology 2022](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2022.852595/full)

### Inferences
**Placement rules** [Inference from the above]:
1. Leg-heavy classes (spin, HIIT, barre, lower-body strength class) go **≥48 h before** the heavy lower day where possible, and never the evening before a heavy squat/deadlift day.
2. The best slot for a leg-heavy class is an **upper-body day** (after lifting, ideally ≥3 h later) or the **day after** a lower day, which also acts as active recovery.
3. If a class shares a session with lifting, **lift first**. Strength is the priority, and the explosive-strength attenuation is a same-session effect.
4. Two separate class days plus four lifting days leaves one full rest day. Doubling classes onto upper days (lift AM, class PM) frees a second rest day.

**By class type** [Inference/Opinion]:

| Class | Main stress | Interference risk with lifting | Best placement | Adjustment to lifting plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pilates** | Core, low-load, low impact | Very low | Anywhere, including the day before a heavy lower day; good on a lift day or rest day | None. May let you drop extra core work |
| **Spin** | Quads/glutes, aerobic + intervals; cycling showed the larger acute strength-endurance hit | Moderate for legs, none for upper | Wed, or the evening of an upper day; ≥48 h before squat/deadlift | Keep lower-day finishers short; skip bike-sprint finishers in class weeks |
| **HIIT** (jumps, burpees, sprints) | Systemic + lower body; also gives plyometric "athletic" stimulus | Highest | Mid-week, ≥48 h from heavy lower days; never the day before | If 2× HIIT/week, drop the power primer and conditioning finishers from the lifting days |
| **Barre** | High-rep, isometric glutes/quads/adductors: local muscle fatigue | Moderate for lower body | Not the day before a lower day; fine after an upper day | Count it as roughly 2–4 extra low-load glute/quad sets; trim 2–3 glute accessory sets (e.g., abduction) |
| **Strength class** (e.g., barbell group class) | Whole-body moderate-load volume | Moderate, systemic | Away from heavy lower days; ideally on an upper day or between lower days | Count it as about +1 set per major muscle; consider trimming one accessory superset that week |

### Gaps
- No studies were found on **Pilates or barre** interference with resistance-training adaptations specifically. Placement advice for those is reasoning from intensity and local fatigue.
- The search summary also mentioned a Lundberg et al. 2022 *Sports Medicine* meta-analysis (type I fiber hypertrophy blunted with running, not cycling), but I could not retrieve a source URL to verify it, so it is not cited above.
- The 24-hour recommendation rests on small, mostly male, single studies. Female-specific recovery timing data were not found.

## Q4. Exercises per muscle and sets per exercise for a 60-minute "few exercises, more sets" session; time per set with supersets

### Takeaway
Use **4–5 exercises + a 5-min finisher**, about **14–20 working sets per session**. That works out to **2 exercises (sometimes 3) per headline muscle × 3–4 sets each**, or about **6–12 fractional sets per headline muscle per session**, under the ~11-set per-session point of diminishing returns. Agonist–antagonist or non-competing supersets preserve reps and outcomes while saving time. Same-muscle supersets cost volume load.

### Cited Findings
- Zhang, Weakley, Li, Li & García-Ramos 2025 (*Sports Medicine*), 19 studies, 313 participants: **supersets vs traditional sets showed no meaningful difference** in chronic maximal strength (SMD 0.10; p = 0.36), strength endurance (SMD 0.07; p = 0.81) or hypertrophy (SMD −0.05; p = 0.87). **Agonist–antagonist** supersets allowed more repetitions than traditional sets (SMD 0.68; p = 0.01, per a secondary summary). **Similar-biomechanics (same-muscle)** supersets compromised volume load. Supersets generally raise internal load, muscle damage and perceived exertion [Moderate] — [Digibug (Univ. Granada) record](https://digibug.ugr.es/handle/10481/102755)
- Weakley et al. 2017 systematic review/meta-analysis on superset vs traditional prescriptions (acute and chronic) — [Leeds Beckett eprint](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/11908)
- Singer et al. 2024 (*Frontiers in Sports and Active Living*, Bayesian, 9 studies): **inter-set rest >60 s** gives a small hypertrophic benefit, possibly through preserved volume load. Arm and thigh estimates favoured longer rest but credible intervals crossed zero (arm 0.13, CrI −0.27 to 0.51; thigh 0.17, CrI −0.13 to 0.43). **No appreciable difference beyond ~90 s** [Weak] — [Frontiers abstract](https://frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789/abstract)
- Remmert et al. 2025 preprint: ~11 fractional sets per muscle per session for hypertrophy, beyond which added benefit is undetectable [Weak] — [SportRxiv 537](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537)
- ACSM 2026 (secondary): strength prescription of **2–3 sets per exercise** at ≥80% 1RM [guideline] — [Rep Fitness summary](https://repfitness.com/blogs/training/acsm-updated-resistance-training-guidelines)

### Inferences
**Time budget per session** [Inference: my arithmetic, assuming a 20–40 s heavy set and a 30–50 s moderate set]:

| Block | Content | Time |
|---|---|---|
| General warm-up | 5 min bike/row + dynamic drills | ~5 min |
| Main lift | 2–4 ramp-up sets + 4 working sets × (≈30 s + 2.5–3 min rest) | ~15–20 min |
| Superset A | 3 rounds × (A1 ≈40 s → 60–90 s → A2 ≈40 s → 60–90 s) ≈ 3–4 min/round | ~10–12 min |
| Superset B | 3 rounds × ≈3 min/round | ~9–10 min |
| Finisher | 2–3 rounds, two moves back-to-back, 30–45 s rest; or 5–6 min EMOM/intervals | ~5–6 min |
| **Total** | **~14–20 working sets** | **~45–55 min (5–10 min buffer)** |

- In a superset round like the one above, each muscle gets **~2–3 min** between its own sets even though the clock only shows 60–90 s between exercises. That is consistent with Singer's ">60 s, plateau ~90 s" finding, and it keeps load high for "lift heavier".
- **Pairing rules:** pair antagonists (press + row/pull, quad + hamstring) or non-competing regions (lateral raise + curl, hip abduction + back extension). Avoid pairing two exercises that load the same prime mover (e.g., hip thrust + RDL as A1/A2, or bench + incline press), because same-muscle supersets cost volume load (Zhang 2025).
- **Main lifts are not supersetted with anything demanding.** On upper days, the antagonist pairing bench + row is acceptable (agonist–antagonist preserves reps). On lower days, the heavy squat/deadlift is done as straight sets, optionally paired with a low-fatigue core or mobility drill.
- **Finishers** should be short (≤6 min) and either a light arms/delt superset (counts as volume) or brief conditioning (swings, sled, bike sprints; not counted as hypertrophy sets). In weeks with HIIT or spin classes, prefer the light-volume finisher over conditioning.

### Gaps
- No study found that directly measures session duration with antagonist supersets in women; the timings are arithmetic estimates.
- The exact per-session ceiling (~11 sets) comes from a preprint, and the true optimal number of exercises per muscle for regional hypertrophy in women was not established by the sources found.

## Q5. Candidate weekly templates (Mon–Sun, 4 lifting + 2 class days), weekly sets per muscle, and recommendation

### Takeaway
**Recommended: Template A, "Themed Upper/Lower"** (Mon Glutes+Hams · Tue Back+Chest · Wed Class · Thu Shoulders+Back · Fri Quads+Glutes · Sat Class · Sun Rest). It is the only design tested here that keeps **2 headline muscle groups per day** while giving **every major muscle 2 sessions/week and ~10–19 fractional sets**. It also puts each main lift's pattern in the week twice and places both classes ≥48 h before heavy lower days. Template B (her own pairing split) is a fine hypertrophy choice if she strongly prefers it, but it leaves upper body at 1×/week. Template C trades some arm and back volume for weekly power and athletic work.

### Cited Findings
- Weekly volume drives hypertrophy with diminishing returns, and fractional counting predicts best — [train.fitness summary of Pelland 2025](https://train.fitness/personal-trainer-blogs/not-all-sets-are-created-equal)
- ACSM 2026: ≥2 days/week per major muscle group; ≥10 sets/muscle/week for hypertrophy (secondary) — [ACSM infographic](https://acsm.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Position-Stand-infographic.pdf); [NFPT](https://nfpt.com/beyond-the-guidelines-what-the-2026-acsm-position-stand-gets-right-and-what-it-changes-for-personal-trainers/)
- Volume-equated frequency does not change hypertrophy, but frequency helps strength — [Menno Henselmans review](https://mennohenselmans.com/training-frequency-2018-meta-analysis-review/); [PubMed 29470825](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29470825); [train.fitness](https://train.fitness/personal-trainer-blogs/not-all-sets-are-created-equal)
- Coach opinion: lower–upper–lower–upper is a common 4-day structure for glute-focused women [Opinion] — [Huberman Lab summary](https://yage-health.pages.dev/articles/exercise/build-your-ideal-physique-dr-bret-contreras)

### Inferences
All templates below are **[Opinion/inference]**: programming derived from the cited evidence, not tested as whole programs. Set counts use the fractional convention at the top of this file. Main lifts: 4 × 3–6, RIR 1–3, 2.5–3 min rest. Accessories: RIR 0–2.

#### Template A — "Themed Upper/Lower" (RECOMMENDED)

| Day | Session | Headline groups |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Lower A: heavy hinge | **Glutes + Hamstrings** |
| Tue | Upper A: horizontal push/pull, heavy bench | **Back + Chest** |
| Wed | **Class 1** (48 h after Mon lower, 48 h before Fri lower) | — |
| Thu | Upper B: vertical push/pull, heavy OHP/pull-up | **Shoulders + Back** (arms finisher) |
| Fri | Lower B: heavy squat | **Quads + Glutes** |
| Sat | **Class 2** (24 h after Fri lower; 48 h before Mon lower) | — |
| Sun | Rest | — |

- **Mon (Glutes + Hams):** Deadlift (conventional, sumo or trap-bar) 4×3–5 → A1 Barbell hip thrust 3×6–10 / A2 Seated or lying leg curl 3×8–12 → B1 DB Romanian deadlift 3×8–12 (swap for a 45° back extension if the lower back is fatigued) / B2 Cable or machine hip abduction 2×12–20 → Finisher: KB swing EMOM 6 min. *Fractional: glutes 12, hams 8, quads 2.* ~15 working sets.
- **Tue (Back + Chest):** A1 Bench press 4×3–6 / A2 Chest-supported row 4×6–10 → B1 Incline DB press 3×8–12 / B2 Lat pulldown or pull-up 3×8–12 → Finisher: lateral raise 3×12–20 + DB curl 3×10–15 back-to-back. *Chest 7, back 7, biceps 6.5, triceps 3.5, side delts 3, front delts 3.5, rear delts 2.*
- **Thu (Shoulders + Back):** A1 Overhead press (standing barbell or seated DB) 4×4–6 / A2 Pull-up or chin-up (weighted or assisted) 4×4–8 → B1 DB lateral raise 3×12–20 / B2 One-arm DB row or seal row 3×8–12 → Finisher: close-grip/deficit push-up 3×AMRAP-2 + overhead cable triceps extension 3×10–15. *Shoulders: OHP 4 + laterals 3 + indirect 1; back 7, biceps 3.5, triceps 6.5, chest 3, rear delts 1.5.*
- **Fri (Quads + Glutes):** Back squat (high-bar or front) 4×3–6 → A1 Bulgarian split squat 3×6–10/leg / A2 Lying leg curl 3×10–12 → B1 Leg extension 3×10–15 / B2 Standing calf raise 3×10–15 (or hip abduction) → Finisher: sled push or bike sprints, 5 min (skip in spin/HIIT weeks). *Quads 10, glutes 7, hams 3, calves 3.*

**Weekly totals, Template A (fractional sets / direct sessions):**

| Muscle | Sets/week | Sessions/week | Within 10–20? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glutes | ~19 | 2 (Mon, Fri) | Yes (upper end; glute priority) |
| Quads | ~12 | 1 direct (Fri) + 1 indirect (Mon deadlift) | Yes |
| Hamstrings | ~11 | 2 (Mon, Fri leg curl) | Yes |
| Back | ~14 | 2 (Tue, Thu) | Yes |
| Chest | ~10 | 2 (Tue heavy, Thu push-ups) | Yes (low end) |
| Shoulders (front + side + rear) | ~13–14 (OHP 4 + laterals 6 + pressing indirect ~3.5) | 2 | Yes |
| Biceps | ~10 | 2 | Yes |
| Triceps | ~10 | 2 | Yes |
| Calves (optional) | ~3 | 1 | n/a |

**Why A:** It meets ACSM's ≥2×/week for every muscle while each day reads as "two muscles". Bench, OHP and pulling patterns each get a heavy day and a moderate day (built-in undulation for "lift heavier"). Squat and deadlift each get one heavy day per week, with the other lower day hitting those muscles via split squats and RDLs. Glutes get the most volume, at two sessions. Lower days are 4 days apart (Mon → Fri), which gives lots of recovery for heavy lower work. **Honest trade-offs:** (1) a third muscle sneaks in as a superset partner or finisher (leg curl on quad day, arms and laterals as finishers), which is what buys the 2× frequency; (2) quads get one direct day. If she wants more quad emphasis, add 2 sets of leg extension to Monday's finisher. (3) Chest sits at the low end (~10); add a 4th incline set on Tue if chest is a priority.

| Day | Session | Headline groups |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Lower A, heavy deadlift | **Glutes + Hamstrings** |
| Tue | Pull, heavy pull-up | **Back + Biceps** |
| Wed | **Class 1** (48 h after Mon lower, 48 h before Fri lower) | — |
| Thu | Push, heavy bench | **Chest + Shoulders (+ Triceps)** |
| Fri | Lower B, heavy squat | **Quads + Calves** (glutes indirect via squats) |
| Sat | **Class 2** | — |
| Sun | Rest | — |

- **Mon:** same as Template A Monday. *Glutes 12, hams 8, quads 2.*
- **Tue (Back + Biceps):** Weighted or assisted pull-up 4×4–8 → A1 Barbell or chest-supported row 4×6–10 / A2 Incline DB curl 3×8–12 → B1 Single-arm cable row or pulldown 3×10–12 / B2 Hammer curl 3×10–12 → Finisher: rear-delt fly + band pull-apart 2–3 rounds. *Back 11, biceps ~11.5, rear delts ~5.*
- **Thu (Chest + Shoulders + Triceps):** A1 Bench press 4×3–6 / A2 Cable face pull 4×12–15 → B1 Seated DB OHP 3×6–10 / B2 Lateral raise 3×12–20 → C1 Incline DB press or dip 3×8–12 / C2 Overhead triceps extension 3×10–15 → optional push-up burnout. *Chest ~8–9, shoulders ~13 (OHP 3 + laterals 3 + face pull 4 + indirect), triceps ~8.* This is the longest session, at ~55–60 min.
- **Fri (Quads + Calves):** Back squat 4×3–6 → A1 Leg press or hack squat 3×8–12 / A2 Standing calf raise 4×8–12 → B1 Leg extension 3×12–15 / B2 Seated calf raise 3×12–20 → Finisher: walking lunges or sled. *Quads ~10, glutes ~5.5, calves 7, hams 0.*

**Weekly totals, Template B:** glutes ~17.5 (2×) · quads ~12 (1× direct) · **hamstrings ~8 (1×)** · back ~11 (1×) · biceps ~11.5 (1×) · **chest ~8–9 (1×)** · shoulders ~13 (1× direct) · **triceps ~8 (1×)** · calves ~7.

**Why or why not B:** It matches her stated preference exactly and is likely about as good for hypertrophy as A *where weekly sets are equal* (volume-equated evidence). It falls short in four places: hamstrings, chest and triceps under 10 sets; upper-body muscles 1×/week; the push day is a 3-group day; and bench, OHP and pull-ups each get one practice per week (weaker for "lift heavier" per Pelland's strength-frequency effect). **Optional "frequency patches"** fix most of this for ~5 min each, though each adds a third muscle to a day: +2–3 sets of leg curl on Fri (paired with calf raise), +2 sets of push-ups in Tuesday's finisher, and +2 sets of pull-ups on Thursday (paired with lateral raise).

#### Template C — "Hybrid Athletic" (2 lower + 1 upper + 1 full-body)

| Day | Session | Headline groups |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Lower A, heavy deadlift | **Glutes + Hamstrings** |
| Tue | Upper, heavy bench | **Back + Chest** (laterals + curl finisher) |
| Wed | **Class 1** | — |
| Thu | Full-body Athletic (power + shoulders/back + light single-leg) | **Shoulders + Back** + jumps/carries |
| Fri | Lower B, heavy squat | **Quads + Glutes** |
| Sat | **Class 2** | — |
| Sun | Rest | — |

- **Mon, Tue, Fri:** as in Template A.
- **Thu (Full-body Athletic):** Power primer: box jump or broad jump 4×3, paired with med-ball chest pass or slam 3×5 (full recovery, ~5 min) → A1 Push press or standing OHP 4×3–6 / A2 Weighted or assisted chin-up 4×4–8 → B1 Reverse lunge or single-leg RDL 3×6–8/leg at **RIR 3** (kept submaximal because Friday is squat day) / B2 Lateral raise 3×12–20 → Finisher: push-up 3×AMRAP-2 + farmer carry 3×30–40 m. *Shoulders 7 direct, back 4, biceps 2, triceps 3.5, chest 3, glutes ~1.5, quads/hams ~1.5.*

**Weekly totals, Template C:** glutes ~20 · quads ~13.5 · hamstrings ~11 · back ~11 · chest ~10 · shoulders ~13.5 · **biceps ~8.5 · triceps ~7** · calves ~3. Every muscle gets 2+ sessions.

**Why or why not C:** It adds weekly jumps, throws and loaded carries, which are direct "athletic" qualities, without a leg-every-day structure (the Thursday leg work is light). Costs: arms fall below 10 sets and back sits at ~11. Thursday is a 3–4-group day, which is acceptable to her as "sometimes full body". Jumps 24 h before Friday's squat should stay low-volume. **If either class is HIIT**, much of C's athletic content is already covered, and Template A is the better fit.

#### Recommendation
- **Recommend Template A for her.** It is the only option that satisfies all four constraints at once: (1) two headline muscles per session; (2) ≥2 sessions/week for every major muscle; (3) ~10–19 fractional sets/week per muscle; and (4) twice-weekly practice of the press and pull patterns, plus one heavy squat and one heavy deadlift day, for strength. Both class days sit ≥48 h before a heavy lower day.
- **Choose Template B** if session simplicity and "body-part" feel matter more to her than maximising upper-body strength. Expect similar glute and leg development and slightly slower upper-body strength progress. Use the frequency patches if possible.
- **Choose Template C** if "athletic" (jumping, power, carries) is a bigger priority than arm size, and her classes are Pilates, barre or spin rather than HIIT.
- **Class-type switch** (all templates): Pilates can go anywhere. For spin or HIIT, keep the Wed/Sat slots and remove conditioning finishers on lower days. For barre, trim 2–3 glute accessory sets (abduction). For a strength class, trim one accessory superset that day or the next.
- **Alternative layout** if she wants 2 full rest days: do Class 1 the evening of Tue (upper day), ≥3 h after lifting, and Class 2 on Sat. Wed and Sun become rest days.

### Gaps

- Her actual class type and any current lifts or weak points are unknown. Final set allocation (e.g., more glute vs upper-body volume) should be set after 2–3 weeks of logging.
