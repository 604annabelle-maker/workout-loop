## 3. Catalogue of dynamic formats: hypertrophy evidence, interference/fatigue risk, placement

### Takeaway
Most "fun" formats are compatible with hypertrophy when they keep the three main drivers: enough hard sets, sets taken close to failure (about 0–3 reps in reserve), and enough rest between sets for the same muscle (more than about 60–90 s). They become a problem when they shorten rest for the same muscle, cut load to "cardio" levels, or eat into the time for hard sets. The evidence is strongest that supersets (especially antagonist pairs), cluster sets, different rep tempos (0.5–8 s) and push-ups/calisthenics match traditional training when matched for volume and effort. Plyometrics and long-length isometrics build some muscle, but less. Circuits, EMOMs, AMRAPs, complexes, sleds, carries, Animal Flow and kettlebell flows have little or no direct hypertrophy evidence. Treat them as conditioning or skill work placed around the hypertrophy core.

### Cited Findings
**Underlying hypertrophy drivers (context for all formats)**
- Proximity to failure: Refalo et al. 2023 (Sports Medicine) found only a trivial advantage for training to failure over stopping short of it (ES 0.19, CI 0.00–0.37), suggesting the relationship may be non-linear. — [PubMed 36334240](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334240/)
- A follow-up series of meta-regressions reported that closer proximity to failure helps hypertrophy but not strength. This is reported by secondary sources. — [SportRxiv preprint 295](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/295)
- In resistance-trained people, 8 weeks of training to momentary failure produced similar hypertrophy to training with reps in reserve. — [Journal of Sports Sciences 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02640414.2024.2321021)
- Rest intervals: Singer et al. 2024, a Bayesian meta-analysis, found a small hypertrophy benefit for rests over 60 s and unclear effects beyond 90 s. For arms there was little difference beyond 60 s. — [Frontiers in Sports and Active Living 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789/full); [SportRxiv](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/395)
- A 2025 systematic review in trained males also found a modest advantage for rests over 60 s. — [medRxiv 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.22.25336351v2.full)

**Supersets (agonist–antagonist and same-muscle/pre-exhaust)**
- Zhang et al. 2025 (Sports Medicine; 19 studies, 313 participants): supersets and traditional sets produced similar chronic hypertrophy (SMD −0.05, p = 0.87), strength and strength endurance. Supersets shortened sessions without reducing volume, muscle activation, perceived recovery or long-term adaptations. — [Leeds Beckett eprint](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/11908/1/SupersetVersusTraditionalResistanceTrainingPrescriptionsASystematicReviewAndMetaanalysisExploringAcuteAndChronicPV-WEAKLEY.pdf); [digibug.ugr.es](https://digibug.ugr.es/handle/10481/102755)
- Antagonist supersets increased reps compared with traditional sets (SMD 0.68). Supersets of similar movements or the same muscle reduced volume load (SMD −1.08). — same source
- Supersets in general produced higher internal load, more muscle damage and higher perceived exertion. — same source
- A 2025 narrative review by Mang, Beam and Kravitz (JSCR) reached a similar conclusion, but volume was not always equated between conditions. — [MTN Tactical summary](https://mtntactical.com/research/research-review-supersets-vs-traditional-lifting-same-gains-less-time)
- The pooled evidence shows more reps and volume with antagonist pairing, not more muscle growth. Some blogs overstate this. — [search synthesis; Lyfta](https://lyfta.app/articles/supersets-hypertrophy)

**Giant sets / tri-sets / circuits**
- I found no dedicated hypertrophy meta-analysis of circuit training. A Brookbush Institute review, which is a coaching source rather than peer-reviewed, reports similar hypertrophy when the main training variables are matched, with sessions about 35–60% shorter. — [Brookbush Institute](https://brookbushinstitute.com/articles/circuit-training-for-hypertrophy-strength-and-power)

**Cluster sets**
- Davies et al. 2021 (Sports Medicine; 29 chronic trials, as reported by a secondary source) found no hypertrophy difference between cluster and traditional set structures (ES about −0.05). — [FitCraft summary](https://getfitcraft.com/science/cluster-sets-research)
- A 2025 study in resistance-trained people, matched for volume and effort, found similar hypertrophy for cluster and traditional sets. — [Eur J Appl Physiol 2025 / PMC12174233](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12174233/)
- An acute meta-analysis of the neuromuscular responses to cluster sets exists. — [PMC6851217](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6851217/)

**Tempo work**
- Schoenfeld, Ogborn and Krieger 2015 (a meta-analysis of about 7–8 studies): hypertrophy was similar for repetition durations of about 0.5–8 s. Very slow reps (more than about 10 s) looked inferior, but the data were sparse. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/271533635_Effect_of_Repetition_Duration_During_Resistance_Training_on_Muscle_Hypertrophy_A_Systematic_Review_and_Meta-Analysis)
- A newer meta-analysis on rep tempo ("How slow should you go?") exists. I saw only its title. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395267527_How_slow_should_you_go_A_systematic_review_with_meta-analysis_of_the_effect_of_resistance_training_repetition_tempo_on_muscle_hypertrophy)

**Isometrics**
- Oranchuk et al. 2019 (systematic review): isometric holds at long muscle lengths produced more hypertrophy than holds at short lengths. Holds of at least about 70% of maximal voluntary contraction were needed for tendon adaptations. Intensity effects on hypertrophy were less clear. A secondary source gives weekly growth figures that I could not verify. — [Human Locomotion summary](https://www.humanlocomotion.com/oranchuk-d-et-al-isometric-training-and-long-term-adaptations-effects-of-muscle-length-intensity-and-intent-a-systematic-review-scand-j-med-sci-sports-20191-20/); [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/isometric-training/)

**Plyometrics, jumps and medicine-ball throws ("athletic primers")**
- A 2022 multilevel meta-analysis (Frontiers in Physiology) concluded that plyometric jump training can build muscle in healthy people across ages and both sexes. The evidence was mixed, and more weekly sessions were linked to larger gains. Search results framed this as Grgic's work, but authorship should be checked. — [PMC9271893](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9271893/)
- The effects ranged from trivial to moderate, and athletes gained less than non-athletes. — [Sci-Sport summary](https://sci-sport.com/en/impact-of-plyometrics-on-muscle-hypertrophy/)
- PAPE: Blazevich and Babault 2019 separate post-activation potentiation (a twitch-force phenomenon that fades within seconds) from post-activation performance enhancement (improved voluntary force or power). Most PAPE studies use explosive tasks such as jumps and sprints. One source reports jump performance enhanced at every point measured from 30 s to 12 min. — [Frontiers in Physiology 2019, doi 10.3389/fphys.2019.01359](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2019.01359/pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336980043_Post-activation_Potentiation_Versus_Post-activation_Performance_Enhancement_in_Humans_Historical_Perspective_Underlying_Mechanisms_and_Current_Issues)
- A BUILD reviewer warns that jump-heavy programming may not suit people with knee problems. — [Imogen Molly](https://www.imogenmolly.co.uk/2020/03/natacha-oceane-build-review-pros-cons.html)

**Calisthenics and push-up skill work**
- Kikuchi and Nakazato 2017: low-load (40% 1RM) bench press and push-ups matched to the same load produced similar gains in pec and triceps thickness, with somewhat more biceps thickness in the bench group. The sample was small (n = 18 men), and summaries disagree on how trained they were. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Low-load-bench-press-and-push-up-induce-similar-and-Kikuchi-Nakazato/0942e6f23e9a50972335e6b6d950b3de10e06ebf)
- Calatayud et al.: bench press and push-ups performed at comparable muscle activity produced similar strength gains (title-level finding). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263548074_Bench_Press_and_Push-up_at_Comparable_Levels_of_Muscle_Activity_Results_in_Similar_Strength_Gains)

**Concurrent conditioning (running/cycling, HIIT finishers)**
- Schumann et al. 2022 (Sports Medicine): concurrent aerobic and strength training did not interfere with maximal strength or hypertrophy compared with strength training alone (hypertrophy SMD −0.01, p = 0.92), regardless of whether the aerobic work was running or cycling. Explosive strength was blunted more when both were done in the same session. — [PMC8891239](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8891239/)
- Lundberg, Feuerbacher, Sünkeler and Schumann 2022 (fiber-level meta-analysis): a significant interference effect on type I fiber growth when the aerobic work was running but not cycling. The overall effect was "relatively small" and the literature inconsistent. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-022-01688-x)

### Inferences

## 4. Variety, enjoyment and adherence: how much rotation helps

### Takeaway
Some planned variation in exercises helps hypertrophy, especially in specific regions of a muscle. Random "muscle confusion" (new exercises every session) does not add growth overall and may blunt it in some regions. Enjoyment and positive feelings during exercise are linked to sticking with it, and that link is strongest when intensity isn't punishing. So the rule is: keep the anchor lifts fixed so you can progress them, rotate accessories on a planned basis, and rotate the fun parts (primers, finishers, conditioning) freely.

### Cited Findings
- Kassiano et al. 2022 (JSCR systematic review, 8 studies): some planned variation seems to improve regional hypertrophy and maximize dynamic strength, while excessive random variation may compromise gains. Commentators describe this as an inverted U. — [JSCR full text](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/fulltext/2022/06000/does_varying_resistance_exercises_promote_superior.40.aspx); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358212528_Does_Varying_Resistance_Exercises_Promote_Superior_Muscle_Hypertrophy_and_Strength_Gains_A_Systematic_Review)
- Baz-Valle et al. 2019 (8 weeks, trained men): randomly changing exercises every session and keeping the same exercises both produced large, significant gains in strength and muscle thickness, with no significant difference between groups. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338197575_The_effects_of_exercise_variation_in_muscle_thickness_maximal_strength_and_motivation_in_resistance_trained_men)
- One review reports that random variation in Baz-Valle reduced rectus femoris growth compared with fixed exercises, while vastus lateralis and intermedius changes were similar. Accounts of this study differ. — [Kassiano review via search synthesis](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/fulltext/2022/06000/does_varying_resistance_exercises_promote_superior.40.aspx); [Evidence Based Muscle](https://evidencebasedmuscle.com/exercise-variation-for-muscle-growth/)
- Fonseca et al. 2014 (12 weeks, beginners): overall quadriceps growth was similar with or without variation. The groups that varied exercises grew all quadriceps heads, while the single-exercise group did not. The study confounds variation with doing 3 exercises versus 1. — [ExRx summary](https://exrx.net/WeightTraining/Research/VaryingExercisesReps); [Biolayne](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-7/exercise-variation-for-strength-and-hypertrophy-change-is-not-always-good/)
- A 2024 Kassiano study (Research Quarterly for Exercise and Sport) found no significant differences in growth or strength between variation groups. — [search synthesis](https://getfitcraft.com/science/muscle-confusion-myth)
- Ekkekakis's work: affective responses (how pleasant or unpleasant exercise feels) depend on intensity. Intensities above the ventilatory threshold, where anaerobic metabolism contributes, increase displeasure and reduce enjoyment. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1533785/full); [ResearchGate, Ekkekakis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5482294_The_Relationship_Between_Exercise_Intensity_and_Affective_Responses_Demystified_To_Crack_the_40-Year-Old_Nut_Replace_the_40-Year-Old_Nutcracker)
- Ekkekakis and Lind 2005 (as cited in a 2025 paper): people felt better at self-selected intensities than at prescribed ones. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1533785/full)
- Affective responses may mediate adherence (Rhodes and Kates 2015, as cited), and lowering intensity reliably improves the affective response. — [search synthesis; PMC8982257](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8982257); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326982946_Affect_as_a_potential_determinant_of_physical_activity_and_exercise_Critical_appraisal_of_an_emerging_research_field)
- Enjoyment (a cognitive judgment) and affect (an immediate pleasure/displeasure response) are related but separate things. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.825738/full)
- People differ in how much they prefer and tolerate high-intensity exercise, which changes how high-intensity exercise feels to them. — [ScienceDirect, Psychology of Sport and Exercise](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1469029218307726)

### Inferences
- "Muscle confusion" is a myth in the sense that randomizing exercises does not add growth. Planned variation (several angles for one muscle, swapping accessories on a schedule) is useful for regional development and probably for motivation.
- Keep the main lifts stable so progressive overload can be tracked. Load and reps on a changing exercise can't be compared week to week.
- Because maximal-intensity intervals cause displeasure, the fun parts should be engaging rather than gruelling: athletic, playful, skill-based or game-like (ladders, partner work, throws, sleds, carries), not to-the-floor HIIT. For people who enjoy high intensity, short hard finishers are fine because preferences vary.
- Proximity to failure carries its own perceptual cost. A 2025 Refalo paper on perceptual responses exists ([EJSS 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ejsc.12266)). Given the near-equal hypertrophy at 1–3 RIR versus failure, leaving reps in reserve on compound lifts is the sensible default for long-term adherence.

### Gaps
- Baz-Valle 2019 measured motivation (it is in the title). I could not confirm the motivation result in this session.
- I found no study testing directly whether fun formats (e.g., gamified circuits) improve adherence and hypertrophy over 12 weeks or more in women.
- The BASES expert statement on affect (Winter 2024) came up ([PDF](https://cases.org.uk/imgs/10604_bas_bases_tses_winter_24___expert_statement761.pdf)) but I could not read it.

## 5. Structuring a hybrid week so conditioning doesn't eat muscle gains

### Takeaway
Meta-analyses show that adding aerobic training generally doesn't reduce hypertrophy or maximal strength. The real costs are blunted explosive strength (worse when both are done in the same session), possibly less type I fiber growth with running compared with cycling, and plain fatigue. So hypertrophy lifting should come first in the week's priorities. Do hard conditioning on separate days, or after lifting, or several hours away from it. Use cycling or sleds around leg days. Keep most running easy, which matches Natacha's HYBRID model of mostly slow runs with some deliberate speed work.

### Cited Findings
- Concurrent training does not interfere with hypertrophy (SMD −0.01) or maximal strength, regardless of aerobic mode. Explosive strength is blunted more when both are done in the same session. — [Schumann et al. 2022, PMC8891239](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8891239/)
- Running, but not cycling, interfered with type I fiber hypertrophy. The overall effect was small. — [Lundberg et al. 2022, Sports Medicine](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-022-01688-x)
- A 2024 review concludes that in most human trials, gains in aerobic endurance, hypertrophy and maximal strength do not depend on the order of concurrent training. — [Medicine 2024](https://journals.lww.com/md-journal/fulltext/2024/12270/optimizing_concurrent_training_programs__a_review.22.aspx)
- A 9-week trial found that the order of same-day concurrent training affected some measures of power, but not strength, lean mass or aerobic fitness. — [PLOS ONE 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0233134)
- Advice to separate sessions by at least 6 hours appears in one review for high-intensity endurance work. Other 6–8 h figures come mostly from blogs, and one trial used 3 h. The evidence is mixed. — [search synthesis; Medicine 2024](https://journals.lww.com/md-journal/fulltext/2024/12270/optimizing_concurrent_training_programs__a_review.22.aspx); [Barbell Medicine](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/concurrent-training-and-the-interference-effect/)
- A meta-analysis on how sex and training status affect concurrent training exists. I could not read its findings in this session. — [Sports Medicine 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-023-01943-9)
- Stronger By Science's research spotlight says "the interference effect is getting less scary by the day." — [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/research-spotlight-interference-effect/)
- Natacha's HYBRID uses mostly slow runs with deliberate speed runs, lets users pick a focus, and adds 15–20-minute running-specific strength add-ons. — [natachaoceane.com (snippet)](https://natachaoceane.com/)

### Inferences
**Example lean-muscle-first hybrid week (3–4 lifts + 2–3 conditioning touches):**
- Mon: Lower A (squat-pattern anchor) + short upper-body or sled finisher
- Tue: Upper A (press/pull anchors) + Zone-2 bike or easy run 20–30 min (after lifting or in a separate session)
- Wed: Fun athletic or conditioning day: intervals, Animal Flow, MB throws, carries, or a sport/class (not to exhaustion)
- Thu: Lower B (hinge anchor) + core/carry
- Fri: Upper B + optional fun finisher
- Sat: Easy or long-ish run, hike, or a "games" session (relay sleds, partner ladders)
- Sun: Off
- **Rules:** (1) No hard intervals or long runs within about 24 h before a heavy lower-body day, and preferably not the same day. If they must share a day, lift first or separate them by at least 3–6 h. (2) Default to cycling, rowing or sleds for conditioning near leg days; running is fine on upper or off days. (3) Keep about 80% of running easy, as HYBRID does. (4) If lower-body performance (load or reps on anchors) drops for 2 sessions in a row, cut conditioning volume before cutting lifting volume.

### Gaps
- The female-specific and training-status-specific concurrent-training findings (Sports Medicine 2023) were not read.
- I found no primary trial directly testing the 6-hour separation rule for hypertrophy in women.

## 6. Practical session templates and parameters to encode

### Takeaway
A 45–60-minute session can be both fun and good for hypertrophy if about 70–75% of the time goes to hard sets near failure (anchor plus paired accessories, at proper rest). The remaining 25–30% holds the dynamic, enjoyable parts: a primer at the start and a finisher, core or carry work at the end. The parameters below turn this into rules.

### Cited Findings
- Supersets and circuits cut session time without reducing hypertrophy when volume is matched. — [Zhang 2025](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/11908/1/SupersetVersusTraditionalResistanceTrainingPrescriptionsASystematicReviewAndMetaanalysisExploringAcuteAndChronicPV-WEAKLEY.pdf); [Brookbush](https://brookbushinstitute.com/articles/circuit-training-for-hypertrophy-strength-and-power)
- Rest of at least about 60–90 s between sets for the same muscle protects hypertrophy. — [Singer 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789/full)
- Training to failure adds little over training close to it. — [Refalo 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334240/)
- PAPE effects on jumps were seen from 30 s to 12 min after the conditioning activity, according to one cited source. — [Blazevich & Babault 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2019.01359/pdf)
- Planned variation helps and random variation does not. — [Kassiano 2022](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/fulltext/2022/06000/does_varying_resistance_exercises_promote_superior.40.aspx)

### Inferences

#### Template A: 55-minute "athletic hypertrophy" session (Natacha-style flavour, muscle-first)
| Block | Time | Content | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Warm-up + primer | 8 min | 3 min ground-based flow or mobility; then 3–4 × 3 jumps or MB throws | Full intent, full recovery, no fatigue |
| 2. Anchor lift | 15 min | 1 compound lift (e.g., goblet/front squat or RDL or DB press), 3–4 working sets × 6–12 | 1–3 RIR, 2–3 min rest. Optional non-competing filler (Pallof, mobility) during rest. Fixed for the block, progressed weekly. |
| 3. Paired accessories | 18 min | 2 antagonist supersets or 1 tri-set (e.g., split squat + row; push-up variation + lateral raise) × 3 sets × 8–20 | 0–2 RIR, ≥90 s before the same muscle works again. Rotate on schedule. |
| 4. Fun finisher | 7 min | Pick one: sled pushes, KB swing EMOM, partner ladder, bodyweight AMRAP, carry medley | ≤15% of session. Low-skill, low eccentric load. Not legs to exhaustion within 24 h of a heavy leg day. |
| 5. Core/carry + cool-down | 7 min | Anti-rotation or anti-extension + a carry; breathing or stretch | 2–3 sets, technique-focused |

#### Template B: 45-minute express
Primer 5 min → anchor 12 min (3 sets) → one tri-set 18 min (3 rounds) → finisher 5 min → carry 5 min.

#### Template C: 60-minute hybrid day (lift + conditioning)
Primer 6 min → anchor 15 min → paired accessories 15 min → 20 min conditioning (bike intervals or Zone 2 on leg days; run on upper days) → 4 min core.

#### PARAMETERS TO ENCODE

**A. Approved formats with rules**

| Format | Slot | Max share of session time | Rest constraint | Effort (RIR) constraint | Counts toward hypertrophy sets? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Straight sets / cluster sets | Anchor | 25–35% | 2–3 min between sets (cluster: 15–30 s intra-set) | 1–3 RIR | Yes |
| Antagonist superset | Anchor (if non-competing) or accessories | 30–40% | ≥90 s before the same muscle repeats | 0–2 RIR | Yes (both exercises) |
| Tri-set / giant set (non-competing muscles) | Accessories | 30–40% | ≥90 s same-muscle; 0–30 s between stations | 0–2 RIR | Yes, if ≤3 RIR |
| Same-muscle superset / pre-exhaust | End of accessories | ≤10% | — | 0–1 RIR | Yes, but use sparingly; never before an anchor |
| Drop set / myo-reps | Last set of isolation exercises | ≤5% | — | 0 RIR | Yes (provisional; Coleman 2023 unconfirmed) |
| Density block | Accessories | ≤20% | Self-regulated | Stop at 1–2 RIR | Yes, if load is maintained |
| Ladder (calisthenics) | Accessories or skill | ≤15% | Self-paced | Last rung 0–2 RIR | Yes, for the target muscles |
| Tempo (≤8 s total per rep) | Anchor or accessories | Any | Normal | 0–3 RIR | Yes |
| Isometric hold (long muscle length) | Accessories or finisher | ≤10% | 60–90 s | High effort, 20–45 s | Partly (treat as half a set) |
| Primer: jumps, MB throws, sprints, a few KB swings | Start | ≤10% (≤8 min) | Full recovery (60–120 s) | Well short of fatigue | No |
| EMOM (power or skill, or alternating muscles) | Primer or finisher | ≤15% | Built in; each muscle ≥2 min | Technical quality | No (unless alternating-muscle hard sets at 1–3 RIR) |
| AMRAP / circuit / complex | Finisher | ≤15% (≤10 min) | — | Never to technical failure | No |
| Sled push/pull | Finisher | ≤15% | 60–90 s | Hard but crisp | No (conditioning) |
| Loaded carries | End | ≤10% | 60 s | — | No (core/grip) |
| Animal Flow / ground flow | Warm-up or cool-down | ≤10% | — | — | No |
| Calisthenics skill work (handstand, pistol progressions) | After primer, before anchor | ≤10% | Full recovery | Well short of failure | No |
| Steady or interval conditioning | Separate session, or after lifting | 0–35% (hybrid days only) | — | Mostly easy (about 80% of runs) | No |

**Global rules:** combined non-hypertrophy blocks (primer + finisher + core/carry + flow) take ≤25–30% of a lifting session. Hard sets near failure (≥70% of session time) are never cut to make room for fun blocks. If the session runs short, cut the finisher first.

**B. Movement-family menu for variety** (pick from each family; rotate within families, not across them)
- Knee-dominant: goblet squat, front squat, hack/leg press, split squat, rear-foot-elevated split squat, step-up, lateral lunge, skater squat
- Hip hinge / glutes: RDL, single-leg RDL, hip thrust, glute bridge, back extension, KB swing (power), good morning
- Horizontal push: DB bench (flat/incline), push-up variations (feet-elevated, deficit, weighted), landmine press
- Vertical push: DB overhead press, half-kneeling landmine press, Arnold press, pike push-up, handstand progressions (skill)
- Horizontal pull: chest-supported row, single-arm DB row, inverted row, cable row
- Vertical pull: lat pulldown, band-assisted or eccentric pull-up/chin-up, straight-arm pulldown
- Isolation/shape: lateral raise, rear-delt fly, curls, triceps extensions, hip abduction, calf raise, leg curl
- Power/athletic: box jump, broad jump, lateral bound, MB chest pass, MB rotational throw, MB slam, short sprints
- Core: anti-extension (dead bug, ab wheel, body saw), anti-rotation (Pallof press, single-arm press), anti-lateral flexion (side plank, suitcase carry), rotation (cable chop/lift)
- Carries: farmer, suitcase, front-rack, overhead, mixed
- Locomotion/ground: bear crawl, crab reach, Animal Flow-style flows, Turkish get-up
- Conditioning: sled push/pull, bike/rower/ski intervals, easy runs, run intervals (HYBRID-style mostly easy)

**C. Rotation rule**
- **Fixed for progression (4–8-week block):** 1 anchor lift per session (about 4 per week covering squat, hinge, push and pull). Same exercise, same setup, logged load × reps, progressed by double progression (add reps up to the top of the range, then add load). Swap anchors only between blocks, and for a close variation (e.g., back squat → front squat), not a different movement family.
- **Rotate on a schedule (every 3–6 weeks, or alternate A/B weeks):** accessories, chosen within the same movement family so each muscle keeps being trained from more than one angle (Kassiano's inverted U: some planned variation, not random).
- **Rotate freely (every session):** primers, finishers, conditioning modality, carries, flows, game formats (EMOM vs AMRAP vs ladder vs sled relay). They are not how progression happens, so changing them costs nothing and keeps sessions fun.
- **Never:** randomize anchors from session to session, or swap hard sets for "fun" circuits when time runs short.

### Gaps
- The time budgets, share-of-session caps and "half a set" credit for isometrics are coaching rules reasoned from the evidence above, not tested limits.
- I gathered no evidence on weekly volume targets (sets per muscle per week). A dose-response meta-regression exists ([SportRxiv 460](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460)) but I did not review it; other researchers' notes should supply volume targets.
- No women-specific trials of these mixed-format templates were found.
