# Exercise selection, rotation and swaps (22-year-old intermediate woman, 4 lifting days/week, ~60 min)

Evidence grades used in every bullet: **[Strong]** = several RCTs or meta-analyses agree; **[Moderate]** = one or two decent RCTs or a systematic review of small trials; **[Weak]** = a single small or indirect study, conference abstract, or conflicting reports; **[Opinion]** = coaching practice or my inference, with no direct trial.

Method note: PubMed, PMC and SportRxiv full texts could not be fetched from this session (network block). Findings below come from abstracts, registry records and summaries returned by search. Exact effect sizes should be checked against the primary papers before they are quoted to the user. Most variation trials used young men. Kassiano 2024 (women) and Enes et al. (women) are the exceptions.

## 1. Consistency vs variation: keep main lifts fixed, rotate accessories?

### Takeaway
Fixed and varied exercise selection give similar overall strength and muscle gains, including in young women. Variation that is planned and moderate (different angles or muscle lengths for the same muscle) may spread growth more evenly across a muscle's regions. Random, session-to-session shuffling is not better and may cost some skill-specific strength, but it does raise motivation. That supports a "fixed main lifts, planned rotating accessories" model, which matches her "some same, some different" request.

### Cited Findings
- **[Moderate]** Kassiano et al. 2022 (JSCR 36(6):1753–1762) is a systematic review of 8 studies (N = 241, all young men; PEDro quality "good" to "excellent"). It concludes that moderate, *systematic* variation appears to help regional hypertrophy and dynamic strength, while excessive or *random* variation may compromise gains. The authors advise choosing variations by anatomy and biomechanics, and warn against redundant exercises and changing exercises too often. — [PubMed 35438660](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35438660/); [LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4077216?lng=en)
- **[Opinion]** Biolayne's commentary on the review: for strength, manage variation carefully on exercises with a skill component (e.g., squat). Varying other exercises will likely not hurt strength gains. — [Biolayne Reps issue 7](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-7/exercise-variation-for-strength-and-hypertrophy-change-is-not-always-good/)
- **[Moderate, women-specific]** Kassiano et al. 2024 (Research Quarterly for Exercise and Sport) compared varied and constant exercise selection in young women over 10 weeks. Muscle thickness gains were similar across muscles and sites, and 1RM gains on the 45° leg press and leg extension were comparable. The authors' conclusion: "virtually similar" strength and hypertrophy. The related thesis describes 70 women aged 18–35 training 3×/week. — [ECU Research Online](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2022-2026/4912); [thesis record](https://educapes.capes.gov.br/handle/capes/983837?mode=full)
- **[Weak–Moderate]** Baz-Valle et al. 2019 (PLoS ONE 14(12):e0226989): 21 resistance-trained men did 8 weeks of either fixed exercises or exercises randomised every session by an app. Both groups made large, similar gains in bench press and back squat 1RM. Vastus lateralis and rectus femoris thickness grew in both. Only the fixed group grew vastus intermedius significantly. The varied group showed a meaningful rise in intrinsic motivation; the fixed group's change was not significant. — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/725137a4c8ff4d64bec855877e948480); [PMC6934277](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934277)
- **[Weak–Moderate]** Fonseca et al. 2014 (JSCR): 49 participants in 5 groups (constant or varied intensity × constant or varied exercise, plus control). The groups that varied exercises (CIVE, VIVE) grew all four quadriceps heads. The constant-exercise groups did not grow the vastus medialis and/or rectus femoris. The constant-intensity, varied-exercise group gained the most squat 1RM, and varied intensity + varied exercise gained the least. Total muscle thickness did not differ between groups. — [Full-text PDF](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Changes%20in%20exercises%20are%20more%20effective%20than%20in%20loading%20schemes%20to%20improve%20muscle%20strength.pdf); [BVS record](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/es/mdl-24832974)
- **[Weak]** Costa et al. 2021 (Int J Sports Med): 22 detrained men, 9 weeks at 3×/week, varied (VAR) vs non-varied (N-VAR) exercises for the same muscles. VAR increased muscle thickness at every site measured (proximal, middle and distal thigh; elbow flexors and extensors). N-VAR did not significantly increase the mid lateral thigh or the proximal elbow flexors. The companion JSCR 2021 paper found similar 1RM gains in all exercises. Fixed exercises looked better for isometric knee-extension strength. — [OUCI record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4Eg3qax9); [Thieme PDF](https://www.thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/pdf/10.1055/a-1308-3674.pdf); [companion strength paper](https://www.scilit.net/article/60844265b9f2608628469e608e3f3b53)
- **[Weak, women-specific, partly conflicting]** Enes et al. (Eur J Sport Sci, DOI 10.1002/ejsc.12042) studied squat variations in recreationally trained females. Quadriceps hypertrophy was similar whether or not squat variations were rotated, which partly contradicts Fonseca's regional finding. — [CiteDrive record](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/the-effects-of-squat-variations-on-strength-and-quadriceps-hypertrophy-adaptations-in-recreationally-trained-females); summarised in [search-sourced summaries of Fonseca](https://www.naseinc.com/blog/exercise-variation-for-strength-and-hypertrophy/)
- **[Moderate]** Strength is highly specific to the exercise trained. In a 21-day study, 1RM rose similarly in a trained arm and in an arm that was only tested (practised the 1RM), but only the trained arm gained muscle thickness. The authors tie 1RM gains to exercise specificity. — [PubMed 27875635](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27875635/)

### Inferences
- **[Opinion, consistent with Moderate evidence]** Overall gains will be similar with or without variation. So the choice can be driven by goals (strength on specific lifts needs repetition), by regional growth (systematic variation helps) and by motivation (some novelty helps). The middle path is the evidence-based default.
- **[Opinion]** Keep 1–2 main lifts per session (the skill-heavy compounds: squat or hack squat, RDL or deadlift, hip thrust, bench or DB press, pulldown or row) fixed for a whole block of 4–6 weeks. Ideally run the same main lift for 2 consecutive blocks (8–12 weeks) while it is still progressing. Most trials above lasted 8–12 weeks with fixed exercises, and strength is exercise-specific, so changing the main lift every few weeks throws away practice. No trial directly tested "4 vs 8 weeks per main lift"; the duration is coaching convention.
- **[Opinion]** Rotate accessories in a *planned* way once per block (every 4–6 weeks), swapping to a variant that loads the same muscle at a different angle or muscle length. Examples: leg extension ↔ sissy squat or reverse Nordic; pushdown ↔ overhead extension; lying ↔ seated curl. Do not reshuffle randomly every session. Kassiano 2022 flags random variation as a risk, and the complaint that sessions were "too varied in target muscles" points the same way.
- **[Opinion]** When a main lift does rotate, change it to a *close* variant (back squat → hack squat or Smith squat; barbell RDL → DB RDL or trap-bar deadlift). That keeps most of the learned skill.

### Gaps
- No trial compares how long a main lift should stay (e.g., 4 vs 8 vs 12 weeks) on strength or hypertrophy. The 4–6 week block and 8–12 week main-lift span are convention.
- Almost all variation trials use men or untrained people. Only Kassiano 2024 and Enes et al. are in women, and I could not fetch their full texts.
- I did not retrieve the ACSM 2026 resistance-training position stand. Check whether it addresses exercise variation.

## 2. Exercises per session and sets per exercise (≈60 minutes, "few exercises done well")

### Takeaway
Per-session volume per muscle shows diminishing returns. Hypertrophy benefit stops being detectable at about 11 fractional sets per muscle per session, and strength saturates much earlier. A 60-minute "heavy and focused" session therefore fits about 4–6 exercises and 15–20 hard working sets. Typical structure: 1–2 main lifts × 3–5 sets, then 2–4 accessories × 2–3 sets.

### Cited Findings
- **[Moderate, preprint]** Remmert, Pelland, Robinson, Hinson and Zourdos, "Is There Too Much of a Good Thing?" (SportRxiv preprint, DOI 10.51224/srxiv.537), is a meta-regression of *per-session* volume. The point of undetectable superiority is about 11 fractional sets per muscle per session for hypertrophy and about 2 direct sets per session for strength. Both relationships are positive but show diminishing returns. The authors caution that there are few data at very high per-session volumes. — [SportRxiv 537](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537); [Biolayne summary](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-36/too-much-muscle-math-finding-the-ideal-training-volume-per-session/)
- **[Strong]** Pelland et al., "The Resistance-Training Dose Response" (Sports Medicine 2026;56(2):481–505; online 2025, DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w), covers 67 studies. More weekly sets produce more hypertrophy (posterior probability of a positive slope ~100%), with diminishing returns. Strength returns diminish more sharply than hypertrophy returns. Frequency has consistently identifiable effects only for strength. Counting indirect sets as 0.5 ("fractional") fit hypertrophy best; direct sets fit strength best. — [sponet record](https://sponet.de/sponet/Record/4097139?lng=en); [SportRxiv 460](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460)
- **[Weak]** Popular figures such as "16 sets" or "6 sets per session" as limits are not based on the Remmert analysis. — [SportRxiv 537 via search summary](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537); [BarBend](https://barbend.com/scientifically-correct-number-sets-per-workout)

### Inferences
- **[Opinion, built on Moderate data]** Cap direct plus fractional sets for any one muscle at about **10–11 per session**. Above that, move the volume to another day of the 4-day week instead of adding sets.
- **[Opinion]** Strength saturates at low per-session volume, so the heavy main lift needs only **3–5 working sets**. More sets mainly add hypertrophy volume.
- **[Opinion, time arithmetic]** Warm-up ~8–10 min. Main lifts: 2 × 4 sets at ~3–3.5 min per set including 2–3 min rest = 24–28 min. Accessories: 3 × 3 sets at ~2 min = ~18 min. Total ≈ 55–58 min. Five exercises and 17 working sets fits 60 minutes. Six exercises only fits if the last one or two are short finishers (calves or core, 2 sets, supersetted).
- **[Opinion]** To fix "messy" sessions, each session should have one clear theme (e.g., "Lower: squat + glutes"; "Upper: push/pull + delts"). Limit each session to **≤3 primary muscle regions**, so per-muscle session volume reaches a meaningful 6–11 sets instead of 2–3 sets each across many muscles.

### Gaps
- No trial directly compares "4 vs 6 exercises per session" with total volume held equal. The exercise count is set by time and volume arithmetic, not direct evidence.
- The ~2-set strength figure for per-session volume comes from a preprint summary and seems low. Verify it in the full text before encoding it.

## 3. Exercise familiarity, learning and adherence

### Takeaway
New lifts show quick early "learning" gains that are mostly skill and neural, not muscle. A few sessions are needed before performance on a new exercise is stable. Familiar, simple-to-set-up exercises let her train heavy from session one, and she can then log real progress against her own numbers.

### Cited Findings
- **[Moderate]** Gains in 1RM are tied to exercise specificity. Repeatedly practising the test lift raised 1RM as much as training did, without any muscle growth. — [PubMed 27875635](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27875635/)
- **[Moderate]** Test-retest work suggests 2–3 familiarisation sessions for adults with weightlifting experience to get stable 1RM values. The number depends on the exercise (3–5 sessions in prepubescent boys). — [SciELO RBME](https://scielo.br/j/rbme/a/w3KnY8pk9fQxy7qV5L39jfP/?lang=en); [SciELO RBME (2)](https://scielo.br/j/rbme/a/SYqFRfzN8XWWqjVcrLN5kTF/?lang=en)
- **[Weak]** In 28 untrained men with a 4-week familiarisation phase, baseline 1RM predicted later strength gains, and differences between responders appeared to be neural (EMG changes). — [UOW record](https://ro.uow.edu.au/smhpapers/135)
- **[Weak–Moderate]** In Plotkin 2023, each group got stronger mainly in its own trained lift (squat 3RM favoured the squat group; hip thrust 3RM favoured the hip-thrust group). This is another sign of strong task specificity. — [Frontiers in Physiology 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)

### Inferences
- **[Opinion]** Treat the first 1–2 exposures to any new exercise as learning sessions: moderate load, RPE ~6–7, and no progression judgement yet. Start progression logic from session 3.
- **[Opinion]** Introduce **no more than 1–2 new exercises per block**, at most 1 per session. New accessories should be machines, cables or dumbbells (low setup and skill cost). Avoid introducing new barbell main lifts mid-block.
- **[Opinion]** Default to exercises she already knows when they appear on the high-value list. Familiarity removes the learning cost and lowers "messiness" (set-up time and reading instructions). Show a one-line cue at most.

### Gaps
- I found no trial showing that *familiar* exercise selection improves adherence in its own right. The link is inferred from the specificity and learning data and from the autonomy literature (section 6).

## 4. Swaps: when equipment is busy or an exercise is disliked

### Takeaway
No trials test swap systems. Best practice (opinion) is to swap within the same movement pattern and primary muscle, at a similar loading potential and muscle length, ordered from most to least similar. The evidence that fixed vs varied selection gives similar gains (Kassiano 2024 in women; Baz-Valle 2019) means a like-for-like swap costs little. Her two disliked exercises have direct, equivalent replacements.

### Cited Findings
- **[Moderate]** Swapping between exercises for the same muscle produces similar overall gains (no penalty for variation in women over 10 weeks). — [ECU Research Online, Kassiano 2024](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2022-2026/4912)
- **[Weak–Moderate]** Squat and hip thrust produced similar gluteal hypertrophy over 9 weeks (HT n = 18, SQ n = 16, MRI). Point estimates modestly favoured the hip thrust, with confidence intervals crossing zero. The squat gave more quadriceps and adductor growth. Deadlift strength improved similarly in both groups. Higher hip-thrust glute EMG did not predict more growth. In practice, squats and hip thrusts are acceptable cross-swaps for glutes, but not for quads. — [Frontiers in Physiology 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full); [PubMed 37877099](https://www.pubmed.gov/37877099/)
- **[Opinion]** EMG differences between exercises don't reliably predict hypertrophy, so swap equivalence should be judged by movement pattern, muscle length and loadability rather than EMG rankings. — Inference from [Plotkin 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)

### Inferences
- **[Opinion]** The **chest-supported dumbbell row** she disliked can be replaced by a seated cable row (default), a machine row (selectorized or plate-loaded), a one-arm DB row braced on a bench, or a Smith/barbell bent-over row. Record *why* she disliked it (chest pad discomfort, awkward setup, or the DB grip), because that rules some swaps in or out. If the chest pad was the problem, avoid chest-padded machine rows.
- **[Opinion]** The **medicine-ball chest pass** is a power or athletic drill, not a heavy strength or hypertrophy exercise. In a heavy, focused session it can simply be dropped. If an athletic or power slot is wanted, offer box jumps, broad jumps, kettlebell swings or med-ball slams, or put "explosive intent" on the first reps of the press. Jump selection for bone health is covered in a separate research note.
- **[Opinion]** Swap reasons should behave differently:
  - "busy": a one-off swap, and the default stays.
  - "don't like": blacklist the exercise, and the chosen swap becomes the default for the rest of the block.
  - "pain / can't do": swap to the lowest-stress option and flag it, without progressing that slot until resolved.

### Gaps
- No studies were found on swap UX, the right number of alternatives, or exercise-substitution rules in apps. The table in section 7 is expert-practice based.

## 5. Best-evidenced exercises per muscle (for the default menu)

### Takeaway
Several well-controlled trials show more growth when a muscle is trained at long lengths, i.e., in the stretched position. Examples: seated over lying leg curl, overhead over neutral-arm triceps extension, the deep-knee-flexion part of the leg extension, and the deep-stretch part of the calf raise. Glutes grow similarly from squats and hip thrusts. For muscles where I found no direct trials (lats, chest, delts, biceps), defaults are opinion.

### Cited Findings
- **[Moderate]** Hamstrings (Maeo et al. 2021, MSSE; 12 weeks, 20 untrained adults): whole-hamstring volume rose +14% with the seated leg curl vs +9% with the prone (lying) curl, driven by the biarticular hamstrings. The authors recommend the seated rather than the prone curl when hamstring size is the goal. (The brief listed this as "Maeo 2023"; it is 2021.) — [PubMed 33009197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/); [Sci-Sport summary](https://sci-sport.com/en/hypertrophy-and-hamstring-protection-seated-prone-leg-curl/)
- **[Moderate]** Triceps (Maeo et al., Eur J Sport Sci, online 2022, print 2023): whole-triceps hypertrophy was +19.9% after overhead cable elbow extension vs +13.9% with the neutral-arm (pushdown-style) position. The authors recommend the overhead position for maximal triceps hypertrophy. — [Taylor & Francis DOI 10.1080/17461391.2022.2100279](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/17461391.2022.2100279); [news summary](https://www.yahoo.com/video/want-build-bigger-triceps-overhead-113000475.html)
- **[Weak–Moderate, women]** Quadriceps on the leg extension (Pedrosa et al., Eur J Sport Sci 2022; 45 untrained women): lengthened partial range (100–65° knee flexion) produced larger distal quadriceps hypertrophy than full range (100–30°). Shortened partials (65–30°) grew the least. — [Substack summary](https://shortcutu.substack.com/p/resist-with-the-stretch-not-against); [FitCraft summary](https://getfitcraft.com/science/lengthened-partials-research) (secondary sources only; primary not fetched)
- **[Moderate, women]** Calves (Kassiano et al. 2023): in young women, calf raises in the lengthened (stretched) partial range produced greater gastrocnemius hypertrophy than shortened-range partials. Sources disagree on the comparison with full range. — [PubMed 37015016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37015016/)
- **[Weak, conflicting]** Range of motion in general: Wolf et al. 2023's meta-analysis is summarised in conflicting ways (full ROM better on all outcomes vs partial ≈ full on average). Wolf et al. 2025 (PeerJ) found lengthened partials gave muscle adaptations similar to full ROM in trained people. The safe reading: full ROM with emphasis on the stretched end is at least as good as anything else. — [PeerJ 18904](https://peerj.com/articles/18904); [SportRxiv 455](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/455)
- **[Weak–Moderate]** Glutes (Plotkin et al. 2023, Frontiers in Physiology): hip thrust ≈ back squat for glute hypertrophy. Squats add quads and adductors. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)
- **[Weak]** Systematic variation of exercises for the same muscle produced growth at all measured regions (quads, elbow flexors and extensors), whereas fixed selection missed some sites. — [Costa 2021, OUCI](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4Eg3qax9); [Fonseca 2014 PDF](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Changes%20in%20exercises%20are%20more%20effective%20than%20in%20loading%20schemes%20to%20improve%20muscle%20strength.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[Opinion, built on Moderate data]** Default menu bias:
  - **seated leg curl** over lying
  - **overhead cable or DB triceps extension** as the primary triceps move (pushdown as the rotating partner)
  - **leg extension** cued to control the deep-bend portion
  - **standing calf raise** with a full stretch and a pause at the bottom
  - **squat-pattern + hip thrust** both included across the week for glutes, with squat/hack squat also covering quads
- **[Opinion]** For muscles without direct trials, choose movements that are easy to learn, easy to load and give a good stretch. Lats: lat pulldown (easier than pull-ups to load progressively). Chest: DB or machine press. Delts: cable or DB lateral raise. Biceps: incline DB curl or cable curl.
- **[Opinion]** Use the "stretch-biased vs shortened-biased" pairs as the planned accessory rotation each block (e.g., overhead extension ↔ pushdown; seated ↔ lying leg curl). This turns Fonseca and Costa's regional-variation benefit into a rule.

### Gaps
- Lateral raise, rear delt, lat, chest, incline press and biceps stretch-position trials (e.g., Larsen 2024 on lateral raises) were not retrieved in this session. Defaults for those muscles are opinion.
- Pedrosa 2022 details come from secondary summaries only.

## 6. Enjoyment, choice and autonomy

### Takeaway
More autonomous (self-endorsed) motivation is consistently linked to exercise participation, and intrinsic motivation is the best predictor of long-term adherence. Giving choice in resistance training (exercise order, reps, load, and preference-based programmes) generally performs as well as prescription and raises perceived autonomy or motivation. Effects on adherence are small or unproven. Offering her options is low-risk and likely helps her stick with the plan, but it is not a performance booster.

### Cited Findings
- **[Moderate–Strong (observational-heavy)]** Teixeira et al. 2012 (IJBNPA 9:78) is a systematic review of 66 empirical studies. It found consistent support for a positive relation between more autonomous motivation and exercise. Identified regulation predicted early adoption, while intrinsic motivation was more predictive of long-term adherence. Most studies were non-experimental, but results were similar across designs. — [IJBNPA PDF](https://ijbnpa.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1479-5868-9-78); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/7f329e2f5a7f4f35bdd468a949a85a40)
- **[Weak–Moderate]** Exercise variation via an app raised intrinsic motivation in trained men, with no loss of strength or size gains. — [Baz-Valle 2019, DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/725137a4c8ff4d64bec855877e948480)
- **[Weak]** APART trial (Halperin group, Tel Aviv; NCT05371587). An autonomy-supportive approach, where trainees choose loads by preference and lift to RPE 7–8, was compared with a standard prescription. Per a 2025 World Physiotherapy Congress abstract, 101 completed. Adherence was similar (69.5% APART vs 71.2% standard), and APART was non-inferior for performance outcomes. Non-inferiority was not established for health outcomes, though improvements were generally comparable. Full paper not found. — [World Physiotherapy abstract](https://world.physio/congress-proceeding/comparing-autonomy-and-perceptions-approach-resistance-training-apart-standard); [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05371587)
- **[Weak]** Self-selected vs fixed repetitions in resistance-trained people (Psychological Research 2020): comparable bar velocity, force and enjoyment. Participants reported greater perceived autonomy when choosing, and preferences were split roughly evenly. — [Springer s00426-020-01402-4](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00426-020-01402-4)
- **[Weak]** Self-selected vs predetermined exercise order in elite youth hockey players (PeerJ 2020): trivial-to-small differences in power output, fatigue and enjoyment. — [PeerJ 10361](https://peerj.com/articles/10361); [PMC7666817](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7666817/)
- **[Weak]** Choice in motor tasks: Wulf & Lewthwaite's OPTIMAL-theory work holds that small choices improve motor learning and performance. Halperin et al. 2017 found kickboxers punched harder and faster when allowed to choose punch order. Wulf 2014, "Choosing to exercise more: Small choices increase exercise engagement" (Psychol Sport Exerc 15(3):268–271), exists, but I could not verify its findings. — [PeerJ 10361 (intro citing Wulf & Lewthwaite 2016; Halperin 2017)](https://peerj.com/articles/10361); [KB citation record](https://ibl.kb.nl/articles/0000000011246/15/3)
- **[Weak]** In a 6-week RCT in cardiac-rehab or senior-wellness participants (n = 30), choice of load raised perceived autonomy and adherence vs no choice, but the no-choice group gained more absolute strength. (This record came from a search summary; I could not confirm which publication it is.) — [SCAPPS JEMS 915](https://www.scapps.org/jems/index.php/1/article/view/915)
- **[Weak]** In an exercise-bike study, the opportunity for choice increased positive exercise experience, attitudes and intentions, but only in people with prior experience exercising on a bike. Attribution comes from a search snippet and is uncertain. — [RTI publication record](https://rti.org/publication/choice-and-perceptions-exercise)

### Inferences
- **[Opinion]** Choice works best as *bounded* choice: pick 1 of 3–4 pre-vetted equivalent options. Unlimited choice conflicts with her "few exercises, less wordy" request. The swap menu should be compact.
- **[Opinion]** A per-block "choose your accessory" moment gives her autonomy without making sessions messy. For example, before each block she picks 1 of 3 options for each rotating slot, and it then stays fixed for the block.
- **[Opinion]** Because performance outcomes were non-inferior and the no-choice group in one study gained more strength, keep load progression rules-based even when exercise *selection* is her choice. Load choice alone may drift too easy.

### Gaps
- No resistance-training RCT in young women tests self-selected *exercises* with adherence as the primary outcome. The APART full results are unpublished as far as I could find.
- Wulf 2014's findings and the bike-choice study's attribution could not be verified.

## 7. Encodable rules for the generator (synthesis) and swap table

### Takeaway
Use 5 exercises (range 4–6) and 15–20 working sets per 60-minute session, with a single session theme. Keep main lifts fixed for each 4–6 week block, and keep them across two blocks when progressing. Rotate 1–2 accessories per session each block, in a planned way. Offer a default plus 3 equivalent swaps per exercise, with up to 6 behind a "more" control. A dislike permanently replaces the exercise; a busy machine only replaces it for that day.

### Cited Findings
- **[Moderate]** Per-session hypertrophy volume plateaus at about 11 fractional sets per muscle. — [SportRxiv 537](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537)
- **[Moderate]** Planned variation is fine and may help regional growth; random variation may hurt. — [Kassiano 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35438660/)
- **[Moderate]** Fixed vs varied gives similar gains in young women. — [Kassiano 2024](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2022-2026/4912)
- **[Moderate]** Stretch-biased defaults: [Maeo 2021 hamstrings](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/); [Maeo triceps](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/17461391.2022.2100279); [Kassiano 2023 calves](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37015016/)

### Inferences (encodable rules; all [Opinion] unless tagged)

**R1. Session size**
- `exercises_per_session = 5` (min 4, max 6; the 6th only if it is a ≤2-set finisher such as core or calves).
- `working_sets_per_session = 15–20`.
- `main_lifts_per_session = 1–2`: 3–5 sets × 4–8 reps, 2–3 min rest.
- `accessories_per_session = 2–4`: 2–3 sets × 8–15 reps, 60–90 s rest; supersets allowed for non-competing muscles.
- `max_sets_per_muscle_per_session = 10` (hard cap 11) **[Moderate basis]**.
- `primary_regions_per_session ≤ 3`; each session gets a one-line theme label.

**R2. Main lift persistence**
- A main lift stays fixed for the whole block (`block_length = 4–6 weeks`).
- Keep the same main lift into the next block if `e1RM` or reps-at-load improved over the block. Rotate to a *close* variant after 2 blocks (8–12 weeks) or after a plateau of 2+ weeks.
- Never rotate a main lift mid-block, except for pain.

**R3. Accessory rotation ("some same, some different")**
- About 60–70% of exercise slots are unchanged from last block; about 30–40% (1–2 slots per session) rotate each new block.
- A rotating slot moves to a variant for the same muscle at a different angle or muscle length (stretch-biased ↔ shortened-biased pair).
- No random session-to-session reshuffling **[Moderate basis: Kassiano 2022]**.
- Max 1 brand-new exercise per session and 2 per block. The first 2 sessions of a new exercise run at RPE ≤7 with no progression judgement.

**R4. Swap offering**
- Every exercise shows `default + 3 swaps` (same pattern and primary muscle, ranked by similarity). A "more" control reveals up to 6 total.
- Swap reason handling:
  - `busy`: one-time swap.
  - `dislike`: blacklist the exercise; the swap becomes the block default and never resurfaces as a default.
  - `pain`: lowest-stress swap, plus a flag.
- Optionally ask what she didn't like (setup, feel, pain) to filter swaps.
- Before each block, let her pick 1 of 3 options for each rotating slot (bounded autonomy) **[Weak–Moderate basis: Teixeira 2012; Baz-Valle 2019; APART]**.
- Already blacklisted: medicine-ball chest pass and chest-supported dumbbell row.

**R5. Default-menu bias** (from section 5)
- Seated leg curl > lying.
- Overhead triceps extension as the primary triceps exercise.
- Leg extension with a controlled deep range.
- Calf raises with a deep stretch and pause.
- Both a squat pattern and a hip thrust across the week.
- Lat pulldown before pull-ups for loadability.

### Gaps
- None of the R1–R5 numbers (exercises per session, 3 swaps, 60–70% fixed, 4–6 week blocks) has been tested head-to-head. They are opinion built on the volume, variation and autonomy evidence above.
- The swap rankings are coaching judgement. No studies rank substitutes for equivalence.
