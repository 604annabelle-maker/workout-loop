# The plan she approved

Agreed with her on 3 November 2026, after planning it together question by
question, and built from this. Where the research reports disagree with it,
this is what the app does.

## Her week

- About 4 lifting days and 2 classes a week (sometimes 3 and 3), Sunday,
  Tuesday, Thursday and Friday; Saturday is usually rest. Sessions go in order,
  not by weekday, so a missed day shifts the rest rather than losing one.
- About 60 minutes, two main muscle groups a day:
  1. **Back + biceps:** a heavy pull-up or row, then a lighter chest press,
     another back exercise and two curls.
  2. **Glutes + hamstrings:** a heavy deadlift, then a lighter squat, hip
     thrust and leg curl, and an **ab finisher** (her change: the plan first
     had an arm finisher here).
  3. **Chest + triceps:** a heavy bench press, then a lighter row, shoulders
     and two triceps exercises.
  4. **Quads + glutes:** a heavy squat, then a lighter RDL, split squat and leg
     extension.
- Arms are the priority, about 12 to 16 sets a week each for biceps and
  triceps. She chose arms over glutes ("I know a lot of women like doing a lot
  of glutes but I like arms more"), so glutes are back to normal, about 12 to
  14 sets a week.
- Full body every other week, replacing one of the four days rather than
  adding one.
- Classes (HIIT, tabata, a strength class) are booked in the same gym app,
  but she sometimes changes her mind, so the app does not track them. She can
  say so in the comment box and the next session eases off.

## How hard

- Main lifts heavy: 4 to 5 sets of 3 to 5 reps, 2.5 to 4 minutes' rest, each
  main lift twice a week (heavy once, then lighter at 2 to 3 sets of 6 to 8).
  She wanted to get stronger and asked whether fewer, heavier reps were better;
  for strength they are, and they are safe and good for bone.
- Other exercises 8 to 15 reps.
- Stop 2 to 3 reps short on squats and deadlifts, 1 to 2 on presses and pulls;
  small exercises like curls can take the last set to failure.
- Every fifth week lighter.

## Exercises

- Main lifts stay the same so strength can be seen going up; the rest rotate
  every 5 weeks ("some same, some different").
- Never anything with a chest pad (chest-supported rows and curls), and never
  the medicine ball chest pass.
- Her saved equipment list stays.
- Jumps once a week, in the warm up.
- Line drawings behind a tap ("How to"), full width, one frame under the
  other; two plain sentences where there is no drawing. (Changed after the
  first preview: the photos, and two frames side by side, were too small and
  cluttered on her phone, so photos are gone and the drawings open on a tap.)

## The email

- Just the exercises and the weekly target bars. No tips, no Sunday email,
  fewer words, and no frame around the email, so a small screen is all used.
- Under each exercise: pills `done · easy · hard · skip`, then `undo` (style A,
  small pills, chosen from three samples), and a "Changed something?" box.
- **Swap ▾** opens in place, with a "did this instead" pill.
- At the bottom: "Anything else about today?" and "All done", which marks
  everything not tapped as done.
- The last tap counts; changes count until the next workout is planned.
- Nothing logged means nothing assumed. After two unlogged workouts, the next
  email asks about them at the top.
- Replying still works as a backup: `done`, or only what was different.
- Progress history starts fresh.

## What each answer does

- **done:** the next time, one more rep; at the top of the range the weight
  goes up and the reps go back to the bottom.
- **easy:** a bigger step: the weight up now.
- **hard:** the same weight next time; hard twice in a row brings it down a
  little.
- **skip:** not counted in the weekly bars; the weight stays. Skipped twice in
  a row, and its swap becomes the main exercise. A skipped muscle is made up
  later in the week only if its bar is behind (her choice: a).
- **changed something** (`20kg 10 10 8`): what she typed becomes the record.
  "Did the swap" logs the swap. Words like "elbow sore" make the coach avoid
  that movement.
- **anything else:** read by the coach for the next workout.
- **nothing tapped:** nothing assumed; the weights stay.

## What was tested on her phone first

Her phone cannot open links. Tested by sending her sample emails:

- A `mailto:` button did open a prefilled reply, but a button that opens a
  separate email loses sight of the workout, so it was dropped.
- Jump links inside an email (three different kinds) did nothing.
- Gmail's dynamic email did work, once she allowed the workout address in
  Gmail's developer settings and an attribute email refuses was taken out: a
  swap that opens in place, pills that save, and boxes to type in, all inside
  the email. That is what the email is built on.
